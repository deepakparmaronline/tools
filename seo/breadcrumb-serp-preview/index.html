What this tool shows

Enter a URL, title and description and this tool builds a visual approximation of how Google may display the result: a breadcrumb-style path built from your URL folders, your blue title link, and your description snippet underneath.

Why the breadcrumb path matters

Google frequently shows a shortened, breadcrumb-style version of a URL instead of the full raw address, especially on mobile. A clean, logical folder structure, like site name, category, page, tends to produce a breadcrumb that is easy for a searcher to scan and understand at a glance.

Tips for a stronger snippet

Keep your folder names short and readable, since they become the breadcrumb labels. Front-load your title with the most important words. Write a description that reads like a genuine reason to click, not a stuffed list of keywords.

FAQ

Where does Google get the breadcrumb shown in search results?
Google can build a search result breadcrumb from your URL path, or from BreadcrumbList structured data on the page. Structured data gives you more control over the exact labels shown.
Why does my URL path show as a breadcrumb instead of the full URL?
Google often replaces a long, technical URL with a shortened breadcrumb style path in the search result to make it easier for users to understand where the page sits on the site.
Does this preview match Google exactly?
This is a visual approximation to help you sanity check your title, description and URL structure. Google's actual rendering, truncation points and breadcrumb behavior can vary by device, query and over time.
How long should my title and description be?
Keep titles around 50 to 60 characters and descriptions around 120 to 155 characters so they are less likely to be cut off, though Google can rewrite either one at its discretion.
