What is Author schema?

Author schema is Person structured data that describes the real human being behind a piece of content. It usually sits on an author bio page, or is linked to from an article's author byline. Google has talked openly about wanting to connect content to real, identifiable authors, and Person schema is one of the clearest ways to do that in code rather than just in visible text.

Why author schema matters for E-E-A-T

Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness and Trust are easier for a search engine to evaluate when the signals are structured, not just written in prose. A name, job title, headshot and a set of verified social or professional profiles in the sameAs field give search engines a machine-readable way to confirm that an author is a real, findable person with a track record, not an anonymous byline.

How to generate Person JSON-LD

Enter the author's full name and job title. Add a photo URL, a link to their author bio page, and a short description of their background. Then add sameAs links, such as a LinkedIn profile or personal website, so search engines can cross-reference the author across the web. Generate the code and paste it into the head of the relevant page.

Where to place this schema

Add it to the dedicated author bio page if your site has one, since that is the canonical page describing the person. You can also add it to individual articles if each article names its own author directly in the page.

FAQ

What is Author schema?
Author schema is Person structured data placed on an author bio page or article. It tells search engines the author's name, job title, image and where else they publish, which supports the Experience and Expertise parts of E-E-A-T.
Does Author schema affect rankings directly?
Author schema does not directly boost rankings by itself. It helps search engines connect an author to their published work and profiles, which supports trust signals used alongside other ranking factors.
Where should I add the generated JSON-LD?
Paste the script tag into the head section of the author bio page, or the article page if you are describing the article's author directly.
What should I put in the sameAs field?
Add links to profiles that prove the author is a real person, such as a LinkedIn profile, Twitter or X profile, or a personal website.
