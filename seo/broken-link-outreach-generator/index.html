How broken link building works
You find a page on someone else's site that links out to a page which no longer exists, usually a 404. You email the site owner, point out the dead link as a helpful heads up, and suggest your own relevant page as a working replacement. It works because you are offering something useful, fixing their page, not just asking for a favor.
Tips for better reply rates
Keep the email short. Confirm the link is actually broken before you email. Make sure your replacement page genuinely covers the same topic as the dead link, not just something loosely related. Personalize the opening line so it does not read like a template sent to a hundred sites at once.
FAQ
What is broken link building?
Broken link building is a link acquisition method where you find a dead or 404 link on another site, then email the site owner to suggest replacing it with a working link to your own relevant resource.
How do I find broken links on other sites?
Common approaches include checking resource and links pages in your niche with a dead link checker browser extension, or exporting a site's outbound links and testing each one for a 404 status.
Should I customize the generated email?
Yes. Use the generated draft as a starting point, then personalize the opening line and reference something specific about their page so it does not read like a mass template.
What reply rate should I expect from broken link outreach?
Reply and success rates vary widely by niche and how relevant your replacement resource is. Sending a genuinely useful, on-topic replacement link with a short, specific email tends to perform better than a generic mass template.
