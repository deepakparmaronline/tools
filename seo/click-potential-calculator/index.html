What this calculator does

Enter a keyword's monthly search volume and the click-through rate you expect at a given ranking position. The tool multiplies the two to estimate a realistic number of monthly clicks, so you can compare the click potential of different keywords before deciding where to invest content effort.

Why click-through rate matters more than rank alone

Two keywords with the same search volume can produce very different traffic depending on click-through rate. A crowded SERP with ads, a featured snippet or People Also Ask boxes can pull clicks away even from a page ranking well, while a clean SERP with a strong title and description can outperform its position.

How to use this estimate

Use published organic CTR studies as a rough starting point, then adjust the rate based on how competitive and feature-rich the actual search results page looks for your keyword. Treat the output as a planning estimate, not a guarantee, and compare it against real Search Console data once a page starts ranking.

FAQ

How is click potential calculated?
Click potential is estimated by multiplying a keyword's monthly search volume by an expected click-through rate for the ranking position you are targeting.
What click-through rate should I use for position 1?
Published CTR studies vary, but position 1 organic results are commonly cited in the range of roughly 25 to 35 percent, position 3 around 10 to 12 percent, and results outside the top 5 drop off sharply.
Why is my actual traffic lower than the estimate?
Real click-through rates are affected by featured snippets, ads, People Also Ask boxes, your title and description quality, and whether the searcher's intent actually matches your page.
Where do I get search volume data?
Google Keyword Planner, Google Search Console impressions data, or a third-party keyword research tool are common sources for search volume.
