What is content decay?

Content decay is the gradual decline in organic performance that many pages experience over time, even without any changes being made to them. It happens as competitors publish newer content, facts and data become outdated, or Google's understanding of the best answer for a query shifts.

How to use the decay percentage

A small decay percentage, roughly under 10 to 15 percent, can be normal seasonal movement. A larger drop, especially past 30 percent, is usually worth a closer look: check the current top-ranking pages for the same keyword, compare depth and freshness, and update your page's facts, examples and publish date where genuinely warranted.

FAQ

What is content decay?
Content decay is a gradual decline in organic traffic or rankings for a page that used to perform well, usually because the content has become outdated, competitors have published fresher content, or search intent for the topic has shifted.
What counts as a significant decay percentage?
There is no universal cutoff, but a drop of more than roughly 30 percent from peak traffic is generally worth investigating, while smaller fluctuations can be normal seasonal or algorithm noise.
What should I do about a page with high decay?
Check whether the content is outdated, whether search intent for the keyword has changed, and how competing pages that now outrank you differ from yours, then refresh the content, update dates and facts, and improve depth where needed.
Where do I get the peak and current traffic numbers?
Use Google Search Console or Google Analytics, filtered to the specific page, and compare its highest performing period to its most recent period.
