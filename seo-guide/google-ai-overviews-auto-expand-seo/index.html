Google AI Overviews Auto-Expand: What SEOs Need to Know

Google has started showing fuller AI Overviews automatically on some searches. The change can push the traditional result list lower, making the search page itself more important to SEO teams.

By Deepak Parmar · Published August 29, 2026 · Updated August 29, 2026

Table of Contents

Quick answer: Google is testing AI Overviews that automatically expand on some searches when its systems decide the feature will be useful. The practical SEO impact is more screen space being used by AI-generated content before the traditional links, so teams should track AI-result visibility and downstream business outcomes rather than treating blue-link position as the whole search experience.

What changed?

Recent reports show Google has begun automatically expanding AI Overviews on some search results pages instead of always requiring the user to open the full AI summary. The Verge reported on August 29, 2026 that the expanded experience can place the full AI Overview above an “Ask anything” field and the regular result list.

Google spokesperson Jennifer Kutz told The Verge that the behavior is triggered when Google's systems determine it will be particularly useful, based on user-behavior research. The feature can also stop auto-expanding after a user has already started scrolling.

Why the change matters

The key change is page layout. When an AI Overview occupies more space before the traditional links, users may see fewer conventional results without scrolling.

That does not automatically mean every site will lose traffic. The effect depends on the query, whether an AI Overview appears, what sources it uses, how useful the summary is, and whether the user still wants to visit a source.

But it does make the search-result page itself a larger part of the competition. SEO is no longer only about where a page sits in the link list.

What Google says

Google has been expanding AI features across Search and AI Mode. Its current Search product direction includes richer AI-generated responses and more conversational experiences. Google's own Search updates also show that AI Mode is moving into more practical tasks, including travel planning, price tracking and hotel booking.

The auto-expansion behavior should therefore be viewed as part of a broader shift in how Google presents information, not as a standalone ranking update.

SEO impact

1. Impressions may become more important context

A page can remain visible in Search while receiving a different click opportunity because the user sees more content before the link list. Search Console data should be read alongside the type of query and SERP features present.

2. Titles and snippets still matter

Even in an AI-heavy result page, users may compare the sources behind an answer. Clear titles and descriptions help people understand why a result is worth opening when they reach the link list.

3. Source quality matters more

If AI systems select sources to support an answer, being a clear and trustworthy source can matter beyond a conventional position. Strong original information, clear authorship, useful supporting details and technically accessible pages all help build that foundation.

4. Query intent matters more than one ranking number

Informational searches, product research, local searches and action-oriented queries can behave differently. A single average position cannot describe the whole search experience.

What to measure now

MetricWhy it matters
ImpressionsShows whether visibility is changing even when clicks move differently.
CTRHelps identify changes in click behavior for important query groups.
Average positionStill useful, but should be read with query intent and SERP features.
ConversionsShows whether organic visibility is producing business value.

For query grouping, use the Google Search Console Regex Generator to separate important query themes and compare them over time.

What SEOs should do

  • Track high-value queries separately from broad site averages.
  • Review pages that already earn impressions but have weak CTR.
  • Strengthen original information that is genuinely useful as a source.
  • Keep titles, headings, internal links and structured data clear and accurate.
  • Compare organic clicks with conversions and assisted outcomes.
  • Monitor AI-result changes before assuming a traffic movement is a ranking change.

Most importantly, do not chase a supposed “AI Overview optimization trick” without evidence. Google has not published a guaranteed formula for being selected as a source.

Bottom line

Google's auto-expanding AI Overview behavior is another sign that the search result page is becoming an active answer interface rather than a simple list of links. For SEOs, the response should be measurement first: identify which query groups are affected, compare impressions, CTR and conversions, and improve the pages that offer strong original value.
