# 6 Orion Browser Alternatives for Your Devices and Extensions

Safari is the closest Orion Browser alternative for an Apple-focused workflow. Firefox suits readers who want a different desktop engine and Firefox extensions, while Brave combines Chromium compatibility with built-in blocking. Choose Vivaldi for detailed tab organization, DuckDuckGo for simpler privacy controls, or Chrome when a required work extension drives the decision. None reproduces Orion's unusual mix of WebKit and Chrome/Firefox extension support on every device. There is also a recent platform change: Kagi has stopped its own Linux and Windows development. Pick by your devices and essential extensions before moving bookmarks.

## What changed, and when keeping Orion makes sense

[Orion](https://orionbrowser.com/) is Kagi's browser, rather than the Kagi search subscription. Its attraction is a native Apple experience with built-in blocking and support for extensions originally written for other browsers. That combination can be more valuable than a long list of generic browser features.

However, Kagi's [October 2, 2026 announcement](https://blog.kagi.com/update-orion-linux-windows) says it is ending direct development for Linux and Windows and opening those projects for community continuation. Development is concentrating on macOS and iOS. The [Linux page](https://orionbrowser.com/platforms/linux) still offers a beta, but now carries the development-ending notice. An available download should not be mistaken for a promise of continued Kagi maintenance.

On Apple devices, keep Orion if the particular extensions you need work reliably and you like its interface. Kagi describes [macOS extension support](https://help.kagi.com/orion/browser-extensions/macos-extensions.html) as incomplete and still in beta. One unsupported API can prevent an extension from working. Its [iOS and iPadOS documentation](https://help.kagi.com/orion/browser-extensions/ios-ipados-extensions.html) explains that mobile support has additional limitations. This is a concrete reason to trial a replacement when a password manager or work extension fails, rather than proof that Orion is unsuitable for everyone.

Cost alone is a weak switching argument. The browser is free. [Orion+](https://orionbrowser.com/orionplus) is optional support, advertised at US$5 monthly, US$50 yearly, or US$150 for lifetime access, plus sales tax. Subscriptions renew and some add-ons may cost extra. A Kagi Search subscription is not required for Orion+.

## Compare the replacement you actually need

This is a research-based comparison of official product pages and documentation, checked October 8, 2026. We did not benchmark battery life, measure tracker blocking, or run these browsers through an extension test suite. Recommendations below are editorial judgments about workflow fit.

All six offer free core browsing. That does not make paid extensions, VPNs, AI services, or third-party subscriptions free.

| Alternative | Strongest reason to consider it | Important qualification |
|---|---|---|
| Safari | Apple integration and Safari extensions | Uses its own extension ecosystem |
| Firefox | Firefox add-ons and broader device coverage | Android and iPhone extension capabilities differ |
| Brave | Built-in blocking alongside desktop Chrome extensions | Changing to Chromium changes Orion's engine choice |
| Vivaldi | Workspaces, tab stacks, and split views | More controls mean more setup |
| DuckDuckGo Browser | Straightforward privacy and data-clearing controls | Verify specialized extension needs before committing |
| Google Chrome | A required desktop Chrome extension or Google workflow | Compatibility is the reason to choose it, not a matching privacy model |

## Safari: the practical first stop for Apple-only browsing

[Safari](https://www.apple.com/safari/) is worth trying first if your reason for choosing Orion was how comfortably it fits a Mac, iPhone, and iPad. Apple documents profiles, Tab Groups, cross-device browsing features, and extensions from the App Store. Profiles separate such things as cookies, history, and extensions, which is useful when personal and work logins overlap.

Safari comes with supported Apple devices, with no separate browser subscription. Some extensions and related services charge independently. Its appeal is an Apple workflow you can inspect without buying another browser plan, not an unverified claim that it will always use less memory than Orion.

Consider a reader who keeps travel research in one group and client work in another. Safari is a sensible candidate if the necessary extensions have Safari versions. If the reader's key tool exists only as a Chrome or Firefox extension, being close to Orion visually does not solve that dependency.

Before switching, make a list of extensions by developer and exact feature. A familiar name in another store may have different functions or billing. Keep Safari on the shortlist for Apple integration; skip it as a universal replacement when Windows or Android must participate in the same workflow.

## Firefox: choose the Firefox ecosystem deliberately

[Firefox](https://www.firefox.com/en-US/) offers desktop browsing, mobile apps, extensions, and built-in tracking protection. Its current product page also describes reader mode and tab organization. It is a good shortlist choice when the Firefox version of an essential add-on matters more than Orion's native Apple styling.

The core browser is free. Mozilla offers other products separately, so installing Firefox does not imply buying a VPN or another service. For desktop work, start with the official Firefox add-on listing for each required extension and confirm that the developer still maintains it.

Mobile needs particular care. Mozilla's [Android extension guide](https://support.mozilla.org/en-US/kb/find-and-install-add-ons-firefox-android) supports installing compatible add-ons and notes that some are incompatible. Do not extrapolate that guide to Firefox on iPhone or assume a desktop extension works on every phone.

Firefox makes sense for someone who wants the same browser family across a Mac and another desktop platform, while accepting a different interface. It is less suitable if switching depends on retaining an exact Chrome-only extension. Try the actual login, download, and document workflows before changing the default. A browser's product-level import promise does not establish that every Orion profile, tab group, or extension setting will transfer.

## Brave: built-in blocking with a Chromium desktop workflow

[Brave](https://brave.com/) is available for Windows, macOS, Linux, Android, and iOS. Its built-in Shields block ads and trackers. Brave's [extension documentation](https://support.brave.com/hc/en-us/articles/360017909112-How-can-I-add-extensions-to-Brave) describes installing compatible Chromium extensions from the Chrome Web Store and reviewing the permissions they request.

The browser is free; optional products such as a VPN are separate. You do not need to buy those services to use core browsing and Shields. This makes Brave relevant when you want blocking built into the browser and your desktop tools belong to the Chrome extension ecosystem.

The tradeoff is that you are moving away from Orion's WebKit approach. Brave's platform range may solve a device mismatch, but it does not preserve every reason an Orion user chose an independent implementation. Also, the cited extension installation guide is a desktop workflow. Do not use it as evidence that a phone will run the same extensions.

For an illustrative workday, try your mail client, a video meeting, and a purchase with Shields at the default settings. If one page breaks, identify a narrow exception rather than disabling protection everywhere. Choose Brave when that combination fits; skip it if WebKit itself is a requirement for your work.

## Vivaldi: replace the tab-management workflow

[Vivaldi](https://vivaldi.com/) is the option to investigate when your browser is a research workspace. Its desktop features include Workspaces, tab stacks, tab tiling, notes, and web panels. These are useful differences for someone who compares source pages side by side and keeps several projects open.

Core browsing is free. The [desktop extension guide](https://help.vivaldi.com/desktop/appearance-customization/extensions/) explains that Vivaldi is based on Chromium and can install Chrome Web Store extensions. It also acknowledges that some extensions may behave differently. Compatibility should be checked with the actual extension, rather than assumed from the store button.

The advantage over an Orion workflow is the amount of organization available without assembling several separate tab-management add-ons. The cost is attention: configuring stacks, panels, and shortcuts can take longer than choosing a simpler browser. Do not mistake that extra setup for a defect if organizing many tabs is the point of your switch.

Vivaldi's [import/export guide](https://help.vivaldi.com/desktop/tools/import-and-export-browser-data/) supports bookmark files and discusses password imports. Its open-tab import and extension import have source-browser restrictions. In particular, Chromium import support is not a promise of direct Orion migration. Choose it for desktop organization; skip it if a minimal interface is your top priority.

## DuckDuckGo Browser: simplify the privacy routine

[DuckDuckGo Browser](https://duckduckgo.com/app) is available for Mac, Windows, iOS, and Android. The product page describes built-in tracking protections, cookie pop-up handling, and a button for deleting browsing data. Its browser is distinct from merely selecting DuckDuckGo as a search engine in another app.

Core browsing is free, while optional subscription protections are separate. The attraction is having common privacy actions visible in the browser instead of managing several extensions. That can suit someone who chose Orion mainly for a cleaner browsing experience and now wants fewer decisions about settings.

Deleting browsing data needs a little planning. Check what the current app's clearing action removes and what saved exceptions preserve before using it during an active work session. A privacy feature can be useful while still requiring you to sign in again afterwards.

This is a weaker first choice if a specialized browser extension is central to your job. This comparison does not establish compatibility with that extension. Verify it on the exact app and platform before moving accounts. Choose DuckDuckGo for a simpler protection workflow; skip it when you need to reproduce a detailed extension setup without changes. Its advertised privacy protections also should not be treated as a guarantee that every website sees no information about you.

## Chrome: let a required work dependency justify it

[Google Chrome](https://www.google.com/chrome/) is a reasonable Orion alternative when the real problem is a work extension, a managed company environment, or a Google account workflow. Its official product page describes desktop extension customization, tab groups, and Google account integration.

The browser is free. Workspace subscriptions, commercial extensions, and other Google services have their own costs. If a company gives you a managed profile, its policies may also determine which extensions can be installed and which settings you can change.

Chrome earns a place here for compatibility needs. It is not an equivalent substitute for Orion's privacy positioning. Before choosing it, inspect sign-in, sync, search, and privacy settings instead of assuming that moving bookmarks recreates the previous browser's behavior.

For a concrete decision, ask your employer whether the required extension is supported in another browser and test that approved option. If support specifically depends on Chrome, keep a work profile there and consider a separate personal browser. You do not have to move all browsing to solve one dependency. Skip Chrome as the first recommendation when the main reason for leaving Orion is to preserve its privacy philosophy.

## A migration plan that avoids losing the useful parts

Start with a small acceptance checklist: your two most important sites, one password-management workflow, one extension, a file download, and a mobile handoff if you use one. These are proposed checks for your own trial, not results of testing conducted for this article.

Keep the original browser installed during the transition. Export bookmarks using the available controls, then import the file into a separate destination profile. Compare folder names and a handful of saved links before treating the move as complete. Do not assume Reading List entries or open tabs are included in a bookmarks export.

Passwords deserve a separate decision. Prefer your established password manager's supported setup when possible. If you use CSV export, treat the file as readable credentials, follow the destination's documented import process, and remove the temporary export after verifying success. Vivaldi explicitly notes the sensitivity of its password export files. Bookmark migration is not evidence that passkeys or account recovery methods transferred.

Reinstall only the extensions that remain necessary. Review permissions again and rebuild custom rules, allowed sites, and keyboard shortcuts where needed. Installing an extension with the same name does not automatically import its local data. Record your exceptions before uninstalling Orion.

Finally, make the new browser default only after it passes the checklist. Leave a fallback for important sites while you settle in. Optional Orion+ billing is a separate account action; changing the default browser does not cancel a renewing membership.

## Questions before switching

### Which Orion alternative is closest on a Mac?

Safari is the first candidate for an Apple-focused interface and integration. Check its extension ecosystem before deciding. The closest familiar browsing experience is not necessarily the closest replacement for a Chrome or Firefox add-on.

### Is Orion Browser paid?

Core Orion is free. Orion+ is an optional membership, with monthly, yearly, and lifetime choices. That means a free alternative should be compared with Orion's free browser, rather than presented as saving a mandatory subscription.

### Can I use Orion on Linux or Windows?

Kagi announced that it is ending its own development for those platforms. A Linux beta remains listed, alongside that notice. For a maintained daily browser, assess the current project's maintenance status instead of relying on old availability summaries.

### Will Chrome extensions work on an iPhone after switching?

Do not assume that. Orion itself documents preliminary mobile extension support with additional limitations. Each destination browser has its own platform rules. Verify the exact extension and phone app before choosing a replacement.

### Does switching browsers also change my search subscription?

No automatic connection should be assumed. A browser, its default search engine, and a paid search account are separate choices. Review each account's billing independently and select the search engine you want in the destination browser.

## Choose one candidate and test the dependency

Start with Safari for Apple integration, Firefox for Firefox add-ons, or Brave for a Chromium desktop setup with built-in blocking. Vivaldi fits organized research, DuckDuckGo fits simpler privacy controls, and Chrome fits a specific work requirement. Keep Orion when it already handles your Apple-device workflow. The next useful action is a trial with the extension or device that caused the switching question, followed by a small, verified migration.
