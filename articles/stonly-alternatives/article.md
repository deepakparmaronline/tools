# 6 Stonly Alternatives for Guides and Support Knowledge

The most useful Stonly alternatives depend on what you want to replace. Start with Zingtree for connected agent workflows, Knowmax for contact-centre knowledge and guided flows, or ScreenSteps for employee procedures and training. Document360 combines documentation with decision trees, but its documented beta restrictions matter. UserGuiding suits in-product onboarding, while Intercom Fin Procedures offers conversational automation rather than the same click-through guide experience. Keep Stonly if its branching guides and delivery fit your support process. Compare author seats, guide views, active users and AI outcomes separately before deciding which offer is affordable.

## What kind of Stonly replacement do you need?

A branching troubleshooting guide, an internal procedure, a product tour and an AI conversation can all help someone complete a task. They impose different authoring, maintenance and billing requirements. Treating them as interchangeable creates a shortlist that looks broad but answers the wrong question.

This research-based comparison checks six relevant products against those jobs. Official pages were reviewed on October 14, 2026; no hands-on product benchmark or ticket-deflection measurement was performed. The comparison order follows workflow fit, rather than an unverified overall ranking.

## Reasons to keep Stonly

[Stonly](https://stonly.com/) brings interactive guides and support knowledge together with delivery to agents and customers. If a guide asks the right question, routes the reader to the correct next step and stays current, replacing it solely because another product has a new AI feature can introduce unnecessary work.

The [current pricing page](https://stonly.com/pricing/) lists Small Business for companies with fewer than 100 employees: unlimited guides and tours, multiple languages, one knowledge base, 4,000 guide views and five team members. Enterprise uses custom plans that can include helpdesk integrations, AI agents, automations, SSO and private knowledge bases. A paid numeric price is not published there.

Stonly also describes a 14-day Small Business trial that downgrades to Basic if the customer does not upgrade. Basic retains guides and permits 400 monthly guide views, five published guides, one language, up to 100 steps per guide and one team member. That is a real limited free option, rather than an unlimited support platform.

Guide views are visits by people outside your team account. The page says exceeding the monthly allowance for two consecutive months can result in an upgrade after notice. Model your customer-facing demand using that definition. It is different from a competitor charging for everyone who signs into your application.

Consider switching when you need a different delivery surface, deeper system actions, a documentation-led publishing workflow or structured employee training. Those are requirements to validate, not evidence that Stonly lacks every related capability. Price the needed Enterprise scope before assuming the only solution is migration.

## Zingtree for connected agent and customer workflows

[Zingtree's current workflow product](https://zingtree.com/en/product/dynamic-workflows) describes a builder for steps and rules, connected systems, AI actions, knowledge assistance and publication into agent or customer-facing interfaces. Its current positioning includes both self-service and agent assistance. Older competitor claims that it is exclusively an agent-facing tree tool are too narrow to use without checking.

Evaluate Zingtree when a support process must do more than show the next instruction. For example, a warranty workflow might retrieve a record, ask the missing question and route an exception to a human. The advantage to investigate over your Stonly setup is the fit between that decision logic and the systems where the agent already works.

The [pricing route](https://zingtree.com/en/pricing) presents Business and Enterprise contexts, with a calculator separating users and usage. It defines a user as an author or agent and discusses sessions/views separately. Enterprise requests a quote. The extracted calculator displayed multiple billing and usage values, so this comparison does not present a single number as the complete plan price. Obtain a saved quote for your author count, agent count, customer self-service traffic and required integrations.

The tradeoff is implementation scope. A connected action needs a maintained integration and a clear response when an external system is unavailable. Ask the vendor to demonstrate the workflow using your required package, including an unsuccessful lookup and an escalation, rather than only a successful path.

Skip this option if your only requirement is a small static FAQ. It is more relevant when you can name the decisions, data reads and actions that justify rebuilding your guides. No claims of guaranteed accuracy or vendor-reported performance gains are treated as independent evidence here.

## Knowmax for contact-centre knowledge and guided flows

[Knowmax](https://knowmax.ai/) combines knowledge search, visual guidance, guided processes and learning capabilities for customer experience teams. Its [Flows page](https://knowmax.ai/decision-trees/) distinguishes manual decision trees with human checkpoints, guided conversational automation and more automated AI answers. That choice is useful when different procedures require different amounts of human control.

Shortlist Knowmax if the support organisation must distribute the same process across agents and self-service channels while preserving a controlled version of the instructions. For a proposed evaluation, give a new agent a device troubleshooting flow and then ask an experienced agent to identify where escalation is required. The question is whether both follow the intended policy without hunting through separate documents.

No current numeric public tariff was verified. The official site invites a connection or demonstration; the attempted pricing path was unavailable. Request a quote covering agents, authors, channels, knowledge modules, AI usage, integrations and content migration. Do not reuse old per-user directory figures as current pricing.

Its potential advantage is the combination of knowledge distribution and different guidance modes. The work to assess is governance: how a reviewer approves a changed branch, how agents learn about it, and how the previous version is retained. Ask which functions belong to your package rather than assume every module is bundled.

Skip Knowmax if you want a verified self-service monthly purchase for one small product tour. It is a better discussion for a contact-centre workflow with knowledge ownership and distribution needs. Marketing claims about reduced handling time or error rates do not establish the result your team would achieve.

## ScreenSteps for procedures employees can follow

[ScreenSteps](https://www.screensteps.com/) focuses on employee procedure guidance and knowledge operations. Its commercial offering combines articles, decision trees, checklists, screen capture and recording, with training and in-app guidance in higher scopes. This makes it relevant when the people following a process are staff learning how to complete work accurately.

The [pricing page](https://www.screensteps.com/pricing) lists Operations Standard from $6,000 per year and Operations AI from $9,000 per year. Standard includes procedures, decision trees, checklists, video recording, change notifications, content certifications, SSO and one custom domain. Operations AI adds capabilities including AI knowledge capture, courses and ScreenSteps Sidekick. Enterprise is custom. The page says displayed pricing reflects annual billing; monthly payment is available on different terms. External knowledge-base use requires a custom discussion.

Choose it for evaluation when a long SOP needs to become followable steps with useful visuals, rather than another searchable PDF. An internal account-administration procedure is a better sample than a generic welcome tour. Ask an employee to find the right procedure, follow an exception path and identify whether the content is certified/current.

The advantage to investigate over Stonly is the fit of its authoring and training process for your employees. The tradeoff is a team-oriented commercial and implementation model. ScreenSteps says it does not offer a single-user licence, and its implementation plans differ in the assistance they provide.

Skip it if you want the displayed operations starting price to include a public customer help centre automatically. Obtain the correct external scope. Also distinguish the offered 15-day trial from a permanent free edition; the pricing FAQ explicitly says there is no free version.

## Document360 for documentation plus decision trees

[Document360](https://document360.com/) is a documentation and knowledge-base candidate with editorial workflows, reusable content and embedded help. It also documents [interactive decision trees](https://docs.document360.com/docs/interactive-decision-tree) that present one step at a time with choices such as buttons, radio buttons and dropdowns. It should not be dismissed as a static-article-only replacement.

Choose it when the dominant problem is maintaining a substantial help centre alongside guided troubleshooting. A team could keep product reference articles and a decision guide within a coordinated documentation workflow. The benefit to investigate is whether that reduces conflicting explanations and improves content ownership compared with your present setup.

The [pricing page](https://document360.com/pricing/) now requests a customized plan rather than publishing the old tier prices found in many comparisons. It lists capabilities and optional AI Premium Suite authoring tools. Get a quote that explicitly includes the required tree module, private/public projects, author seats, languages and AI features. A feature inventory is not proof that every quoted plan includes everything.

The important tradeoffs are unusually concrete. The [tree-management documentation](https://docs.document360.com/docs/managing-interactive-decision-trees) describes the feature as beta and lists restrictions: no tree import/export, no step-level analytics, no bulk operations, no videos in tree steps and no recovery of deleted trees. It also says only one user can edit a tree at a time. Those constraints make a representative rebuild essential before moving complex Stonly guides.

Skip Document360 for this job if your migration requires direct tree import or detailed path analytics today. Article import features do not establish decision-tree import support. Conflicting documentation about video support was resolved conservatively using the explicit current limitations list, rather than promising it works.

## UserGuiding for contextual product onboarding

[UserGuiding](https://userguiding.com/) provides product tours, hotspots/tooltips, checklists, resource centres, surveys and knowledge-base features. This is a different emphasis from an agent troubleshooting tree: it helps a user discover and complete actions inside a product interface.

Shortlist it when the requirement is a contextual onboarding experience, such as helping a new account set up its first workspace. Its potential advantage over a separate support guide is delivery around the interface the user is operating. Validate the actual application, including single-page navigation and changing elements, instead of assuming a tour will attach correctly to every screen.

The [current pricing page](https://userguiding.com/pricing) shows Starter from $249 per month billed annually, $2,988 per year, at 2,000 monthly active users. Growth starts at $499 per month annually, $5,988 per year, at the same starting MAU scope. Higher volumes need a quote. MAU refers to people signing into the platform within the last 30 days. The comparison also lists active limits, including 15 guides, 10 hotspots and one checklist for Starter. Do not call these unlimited onboarding experiences.

The tradeoff is paying for the application population rather than only readers of a particular guide. A largely dormant help widget and a widely used app can have very different costs under guide-view and MAU models. There are also Growth/Enterprise dependencies for features such as private knowledge, permissions and SSO.

Skip it if your essential requirement is a like-for-like connected call-centre decision process. It belongs on this shortlist for the onboarding part of Stonly's scope. A “Start for Free” button alone does not prove a permanent free production plan, and no such broad free-plan claim is made here.

## Intercom Fin Procedures for conversational automation

[Fin Procedures](https://www.intercom.com/help/en/articles/12495167-fin-procedures-explained) combine natural-language instructions with conditions, code and data access to handle processes such as damaged-order queries or account troubleshooting. The documentation describes handoffs and simulation tools, as well as procedures that can revisit or skip steps as a conversation changes.

Consider this when you want customers to describe the problem in a conversation and an agent to perform eligible actions, rather than manually click through a guide. A good proposed evaluation is a returns request where the customer changes an important detail midway through. Check whether the correct policy still applies and whether an ineligible action reliably hands off.

[Intercom pricing](https://www.intercom.com/pricing) lists Fin from $0.99 per outcome. Its definition includes explicit resolution, no further help after a response, or a completed Procedure including handoffs; it charges once per conversation. Fin can be bought for an existing supported helpdesk with a minimum commitment. Using the Intercom platform also adds seat and feature-plan costs. Advanced/Expert scopes and optional proactive support features require separate attention.

The advantage is conversational handling and system access. The tradeoff is changing the interface and the billable unit. A handoff can count as an outcome under the stated definition, so “resolved ticket” and “billable outcome” are not safe synonyms. Do not budget Fin by simply multiplying the number of fully resolved support tickets.

Skip this if you need an exact static guide path that every reader sees identically. Procedures offer a different operating model. Validate both intended triggering and the action flow; a successful simulation of a procedure does not by itself prove it will be selected for the right customer message.

## Preserve the logic, not just the words

Build a content inventory before switching: public articles, private agent instructions, guide branches, variables, embedded entry points, translations and escalation actions. [Stonly pricing](https://stonly.com/pricing/) lists PDF guide export, but a PDF is a human-readable reference rather than proof of a portable executable tree.

Choose a guide with an ordinary path, an exception and a handoff. Rebuild it in the proposed destination and record what must be re-authored. Then change one policy shared by two guides. Can the content owner find both places, update the reference and preserve an understandable revision history?

Check customer entry points separately. A help-centre URL, an embedded widget, a helpdesk action and a tour trigger may require different replacements. For public documentation moves, the [redirect map checker](/seo/redirect-map-checker) can help review old-to-new URL mappings. It checks routing plans, not guide logic or integration compatibility.

Measure the purchase using your own workload: authors, working agents, external readers, application MAU and expected AI outcomes. Add translation review, content rebuilding, integration maintenance and overlapping subscriptions. Ask for a written scope with those units so a seemingly low starting price does not hide a different population or missing module.

## Frequently asked questions

### Which options are closest to Stonly's branching guidance?

Zingtree and Knowmax deserve early evaluation for guided agent/customer processes. ScreenSteps is relevant for employee procedures. Document360 also has trees, but its documented beta and migration restrictions may matter more than the presence of the feature alone.

### Does Stonly have a free plan?

Its official pricing FAQ describes Basic after the trial: 400 guide views per month, five published guides in one language, up to 100 steps each and one team member. That is suitable for a limited use case, rather than an unlimited help centre.

### Is a product tour a decision-tree replacement?

Only for an appropriate workflow. A tour highlights and explains an interface, while troubleshooting may need branching questions, data lookups and escalation. UserGuiding is included for contextual onboarding, with that scope distinction made explicit.

### Can Stonly guides be imported into Document360 trees?

Do not assume so. Document360's current management documentation says decision-tree import/export is not supported. Article imports and PDF exports are different capabilities. Plan a representative manual rebuild and confirm current restrictions directly.

### Why is Fin's outcome price not directly comparable with guide views?

They count different events. Fin's stated outcome definition can include a completed Procedure with a handoff, while Stonly views count guide visits by people outside the team account. Model each definition against the same real support workload.

### Should AI-generated procedures replace editorial review?

No. Treat generated material as a draft. A process owner still needs to verify policy, variables, exceptions, translations and action permissions. Use a proposed evaluation with failure paths before turning on a new workflow for customers.

## Start with one procedure worth improving

Keep Stonly when its current guides serve the workflow well. For a change, pick two candidates matched to the delivery surface: Zingtree/Knowmax for connected guidance, ScreenSteps for employee procedures, Document360 for documentation with qualified trees, UserGuiding for onboarding, or Fin for conversation-led automation. Rebuild one representative flow, check its exceptions and total commercial scope, then decide whether a broader migration has a clear benefit.
