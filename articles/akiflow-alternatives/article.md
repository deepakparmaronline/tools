# 6 Akiflow Alternatives for Tasks and Time Blocking

Sunsama is a useful Akiflow alternative when you want guided daily planning, while Motion suits readers who want a scheduler to organize tasks around deadlines. Reclaim is worth considering for protecting time in an existing Google or Outlook calendar. Morgen fits mixed calendar accounts. Todoist and TickTick replace task management more directly than Akiflow’s broader capture workflow. Choose according to the part you actually need to replace. A cheaper task list can become more work if you must manually copy assignments out of several other tools.

## Decide what you are replacing

[Akiflow](https://akiflow.com/) brings tasks and meetings into a planning workspace. Its current site also advertises automatic time blocking, so describing it as a strictly manual planner would be misleading. The useful comparison is how much scheduling authority you want to delegate and which incoming tasks must stay connected to their original system.

The [pricing page](https://akiflow.com/pricing) displays Pro at $34 a month on monthly billing or $19 a month billed yearly, equivalent to $228 for the year. It advertises a seven-day trial. Those subscription costs make a smaller tool worth evaluating if you only use a fraction of the workspace.

Keep Akiflow if fast capture, calendar access and your existing integrations already save you repeated copying. Switching merely to get a different calendar appearance can sacrifice the part of the product doing useful work. First write down your indispensable task sources, your calendar provider and the behavior you expect after an unfinished task misses its time block.

This is a research comparison using official product and pricing pages. Selection favors useful substitutes for a defined workflow. It does not report hands-on testing, performance measurements or a universal winner.

## Sunsama: replace rapid triage with a planning routine

[Sunsama](https://www.sunsama.com/) emphasizes choosing a realistic day, bringing tasks together, timeboxing and finishing with a shutdown routine. It suits someone who can collect work quickly but keeps committing to more than fits around meetings.

For example, a consultant could bring selected client tasks into a daily plan, estimate their duration and reduce the list when the calendar is already full. That is an evaluation scenario, not a measured improvement. Its advantage over a capture-led workflow is the deliberate commitment step.

[Sunsama Pro](https://www.sunsama.com/pricing) is US$22 per month billed monthly or $17 per month billed yearly, an annual total of $204. The product page advertises a 14-day trial without a card. A trial is not a permanent free tier.

Choose it when planning and shutdown are habits you want the product to support. Skip it when the main requirement is processing incoming work with as little daily review as possible. Before moving, check what happens when a source task is completed or rescheduled from Sunsama and whether that matches your team’s expectations.

## Motion: make scheduling part of project execution

[Motion](https://www.usemotion.com/) combines AI calendar planning with tasks and projects. It is relevant when your problem is rebuilding a schedule after priorities or deadlines change, particularly if project work should live in the same workspace.

The tradeoff is adopting a broader working system. Bringing every task into another project manager can create a second source of truth if your team still assigns work elsewhere. An attractive generated plan does not resolve who owns the task or which deadline is authoritative.

The [pricing page](https://www.usemotion.com/pricing) lists paid seat plans and monthly AI credit allowances. It also offers individual/team and monthly/annual selectors. The research extraction did not reliably establish the selected combination, so this article does not present a number as a like-for-like quote.

Choose Motion if you want task scheduling and project planning together. Skip it if you only need a lightweight layer above existing task sources. In a trial, enter a real deadline and duration, then inspect how an added meeting changes the plan. Confirm credits, billing term and required permissions before committing.

## Reclaim: protect time on the calendar you already use

[Reclaim](https://reclaim.ai/) works with Google Calendar and Outlook. Its focus-time and habit scheduling make it a relevant alternative when meetings repeatedly displace important work.

That is a narrower substitution than rebuilding an Akiflow inbox. A recurring exercise habit or weekly writing block can be valuable without importing every project assignment. Choose Reclaim for that calendar problem rather than expecting every Akiflow capture connection to have an equivalent.

The current [Lite plan](https://reclaim.ai/pricing) is free, with a one-week scheduling range, one Calendar Sync and one Scheduling Link. Its integrations are limited; the page places connectors such as Todoist, Asana, ClickUp, Jira and Linear on paid plans. Paid team scheduling can introduce additional attendee terms, so inspect the current quote.

Skip it if your indispensable workflow is collecting tasks from many unrelated sources in one inbox. During evaluation, check the calendars it writes to, what a flexible block communicates to colleagues and whether you can reserve time without creating confusing duplicate events.

## Morgen: put mixed calendar accounts first

[Morgen](https://www.morgen.so/) brings Google, Outlook, Apple and Fastmail calendars into one workspace and lets users plan tasks beside them. This matters when the calendar-provider mix is the constraint, rather than the speed of capturing another task.

The [individual subscription](https://www.morgen.so/pricing) displays $30 per month on monthly billing or $15 per month billed yearly, an annual total of $180. A 14-day trial is advertised. Do not substitute the lower team seat rate for an individual quote: team pricing has a minimum seat requirement.

Choose Morgen if you need to understand availability across accounts and want scheduling links in the same calendar workspace. The advantage is the documented calendar coverage, not proof that every task connector synchronizes identically to Akiflow.

Skip it if a specific Akiflow capture shortcut is the entire reason you use the product. Test an imported task, a recurring event and a reschedule on each provider you actually use. Seeing a task on a calendar does not establish that completion and due-date edits travel back to its source.

## Todoist: simplify the authoritative task list

[Todoist](https://www.todoist.com/) is a task manager rather than an equivalent collection layer for every Akiflow source. It makes sense if you are willing to keep work in one task system and use projects, priorities and filters to decide what matters.

The [free Beginner plan](https://www.todoist.com/pricing) includes five personal projects. Pro adds calendar layout and task duration. The public page did not expose a dependable paid currency amount in this research, so confirm the quote for your region and billing cycle.

An example is a freelancer whose assignments are already maintained in Todoist. Removing the additional planner can reduce the number of places requiring attention. That benefit depends on maintaining one authoritative list; it is not a claim about measured time savings.

Skip Todoist as a full substitute if retaining Akiflow’s aggregation across several team systems is essential. Evaluate calendar integration separately from the paid calendar layout, and check project import formats before transferring historical work.

## TickTick: combine personal tasks with focus habits

[TickTick](https://ticktick.com/) combines task lists, habit tracking, Pomodoro tools and calendar views. It suits readers who want their personal planning and focus routine in one application, without buying a broad work aggregation system.

Its [free tier](https://ticktick.com/about/upgrade) lists nine lists and 99 tasks per list. Premium is US$49.99 per year and adds multiple calendar views and task duration. Do not judge the free plan as if it includes every paid planning feature.

Choose it for a personal list that includes errands, study work and recurring habits. The advantage over a broader planner is that those activities can share a task system. It does not demonstrate equivalent two-way import from every application connected to Akiflow.

Skip it if deep project-system synchronization is the non-negotiable requirement. Test recurring-task behavior, reminders on your actual phone and any calendar subscription you need. App-store billing and platform details can differ from a website quote.

## Switch one source and one week of work first

A calendar event and a task are different records. Preserving an event does not preserve its task notes, source link, completion history or priority.

1. List which application owns each task and which calendars may receive planning blocks.
2. Preserve original tasks in their source systems. Save standalone Akiflow notes separately if you cannot verify a transferable export.
3. Reconnect one task source and one calendar in the candidate.
4. Check completion, recurring work, a changed deadline and a moved time block in both applications.
5. Run only one automatic scheduler against the same writable calendar during evaluation.
6. Review the plan after a realistic interrupted day before moving the rest.

A universal import route from Akiflow into these products was not verified. Treat reconnecting sources and rebuilding personal scheduling preferences as migration work. If distractions are the remaining issue, the separate [Freedom alternatives guide](/alternatives/freedom-alternatives/) addresses blocking tools that can complement a planner.

## Questions before cancelling Akiflow

### Which alternative has a permanent free plan?

Reclaim Lite, Todoist Beginner and TickTick Free do. Their restrictions differ. None should be assumed to reproduce the complete Akiflow workflow for free.

### Is Sunsama less expensive than Akiflow?

The verified public monthly and annual-equivalent rates are lower on the stated comparison date. Feature fit and annual commitment still matter; a lower subscription does not establish a better choice.

### Should I choose Motion for automatic scheduling?

Shortlist it if automatic task planning is the desired change. Test how it handles interruptions and existing project ownership. Akiflow now also advertises automation, so compare behavior rather than relying on an old manual-versus-automatic label.

### Can I keep my existing task manager?

Calendar planning products may connect to it, depending on the specific connector and plan. Verify two-way edits and completion. Todoist and TickTick are better considered the task system itself.

### Will my old time blocks transfer?

Calendar-provider events may remain where they are stored, but task links and planner-specific settings are separate. An automatic transfer of all those relationships was not verified.

Start with Sunsama for guided planning, Reclaim for calendar protection, or Morgen for mixed accounts. Consider Motion when adopting broader automatic project planning is acceptable. Choose Todoist or TickTick when simplifying task ownership is the real goal, and keep Akiflow if its capture connections remain indispensable.
