# 6 Sunsama Alternatives for More Flexible Daily Planning

Ellie is a sensible Sunsama alternative if you want a daily board and timeboxing with less emphasis on guided rituals. Amazing Marvin suits people who want to configure their planning workflow. Akiflow is worth evaluating for fast capture, while Motion and Reclaim address different kinds of scheduling automation. TickTick fits a smaller personal task budget. Keep Sunsama if its daily review, shutdown and workload decisions are helping you work realistically. Before switching, identify whether you want a different planning habit or simply a lower subscription.

## Check whether a setting change solves the problem

[Sunsama](https://www.sunsama.com/) is organized around choosing the day deliberately and reviewing how it went. That can justify keeping it even when another application offers more features.

Its [account settings](https://help.sunsama.com/docs/settings/user-settings/) let you disable automatic ritual prompts and configure task rollover. They also document auto-rescheduling. Try those controls before moving because a replacement is a larger change than adjusting an intrusive prompt.

The [current Pro price](https://www.sunsama.com/pricing) is US$22 monthly or US$17 per month billed yearly, equivalent to US$204 for the year. The 14-day trial is not a permanent free plan. Cost is a valid switching reason when the planning routine is not worth that recurring commitment to you.

Be careful with dated comparisons. The [current FAQ](https://help.sunsama.com/docs/faq/faq/) supports Google, Outlook and Apple calendar accounts. This guide does not recommend switching merely to obtain a feature Sunsama already documents.

## A simpler day board: Ellie

[Ellie](https://ellieplanner.com/) separates unscheduled tasks in a Brain Dump from work chosen for a day. That is a useful fit when you like Sunsama’s daily focus but prefer to set your own review process.

A writer could capture every proposed edit without dating it, choose the few changes that belong in today’s work and then put those beside meetings. The practical advantage is choosing a smaller daily commitment without needing to adopt an elaborate new project system.

[Ellie Free](https://ellieplanner.com/pricing) includes unlimited tasks and Brain Dump. Timebox mode, calendar connections and recurring tasks belong to Pro. The page displays $9.99 a month and a $299.99 lifetime offer. Confirm the subscription selector at checkout; no annual total is inferred here.

Choose Ellie if the daily board is what you value. Skip its free tier as a complete free timeboxing replacement because the relevant calendar features are paid. Test your required calendar provider and recurring-work behavior. Paying once can reduce recurring billing, but it does not guarantee future support or policies.

## A workflow you can configure: Amazing Marvin

[Amazing Marvin](https://amazingmarvin.com/) combines task management and day planning with configurable strategies, time estimates and focus tools. It is relevant when Sunsama’s structure feels too prescribed and you can identify the particular controls you want.

The advantage is flexibility. For example, you could build a day around task estimates and a focused session without reproducing an entire morning interview. The tradeoff is deciding which features to enable and keeping that setup understandable.

Its [annual offer](https://amazingmarvin.com/pricing/) is US$96 a year, shown as US$8 per month billed annually. A 14-day trial is advertised without a card. That trial should not be described as an ongoing free version.

Choose Marvin when adjusting the workflow itself is a positive part of your planning process. Skip it if configuring another productivity system is likely to become a distraction. Start with one strategy and one ordinary workday. Do not enable every available feature simply because the subscription includes it, and check your calendar sync needs before migrating.

## A capture-oriented workspace: Akiflow

[Akiflow’s documented features](https://akiflow.com/pricing) include quick capture, keyboard shortcuts, task integrations and time blocking. It suits a reader whose work arrives from many places and who wants to process those inputs quickly.

Its potential advantage over a ritual-centered routine is the emphasis on handling incoming work. The relevant trial question is whether capture and rescheduling feel easier with your actual task sources, rather than whether a promotional screenshot looks cleaner.

The public rates are $34 monthly or $19 per month billed yearly. On the checked date it is not a lower-cost substitute for Sunsama at the stated billing terms.

Choose Akiflow for a change in workflow, not to save subscription money. Skip it if you are satisfied with Sunsama’s capture and your only issue is price. Test source links and completion synchronization carefully. The product’s current automation features also make old descriptions of it as exclusively manual unreliable.

## Automatic planning with project ownership: Motion

[Motion](https://www.usemotion.com/) combines task planning, calendars and project work. It deserves a trial if you want the application to do more of the scheduling work and you are comfortable adopting a broader workspace.

Its advantage is connecting scheduling with the tasks held in that system. A small agency could evaluate a project deadline and the individual work needed to reach it together. That example does not establish measured delivery improvements.

The [pricing page](https://www.usemotion.com/pricing) has individual/team and billing selectors alongside seat plans and AI credits. A reliably comparable numeric quote was not available from the extracted selector state. Confirm the actual term and the credit allowance for your proposed use.

Choose Motion if broader project planning is part of the change you want. Skip it when your team’s assignments must remain entirely in another project manager and an additional authoritative list would be confusing. Test an interrupted day and an overdue task, and confirm how the plan distinguishes work preferences from immovable client deadlines.

## Protect the existing calendar: Reclaim

[Reclaim](https://reclaim.ai/) schedules focus time and habits around Google or Outlook calendar events. It is useful if your Sunsama routine is adequate but calendar fragmentation still prevents planned work.

Its advantage is treating protection of time as an ongoing calendar problem. You could reserve a recurring writing habit without moving your entire task history into a different daily planner.

The [free Lite plan](https://reclaim.ai/pricing) exists permanently, with a limited scheduling range and connector access. Wider task integrations require paid plans. Check the current paid terms and meeting-attendee costs for team use rather than assuming the free experience covers a company rollout.

Choose Reclaim if calendar protection is the outcome. Skip it as a full substitute for Sunsama’s historical daily review. During evaluation, inspect event visibility and what colleagues see as available time. Using it alongside another scheduler requires deliberate calendar ownership; two services rearranging the same blocks can obscure which plan to trust.

## Personal planning at an annual task-app price: TickTick

[TickTick](https://ticktick.com/) brings tasks, habits and focus timers together. It fits a reader who needs to organize personal work but does not need Sunsama’s exact reflection and shutdown process.

Its [Premium subscription](https://ticktick.com/about/upgrade) is US$49.99 per year. Multiple calendar views and task duration are paid features. The free version is a real free tier, but evaluating it without those planning features can give the wrong impression of the paid product.

The advantage is combining a personal task list with habits and focus sessions. A student might use one list for coursework and a recurring habit for revision, while keeping scheduled classes in the calendar.

Choose it when that smaller system is sufficient. Skip it if your indispensable requirement is Sunsama-style imported work and daily history. Verify reminders on your actual device and the effect of moving recurring tasks. A task subscription and editable event synchronization are different capabilities; confirm what your calendar connection supports.

## Preserve your review history before rebuilding the plan

Sunsama’s [export setting](https://help.sunsama.com/docs/settings/user-settings/) generates CSV or JSON task data from the primary workspace. It includes information such as notes, dates, subtasks, channels and planned time. An export is a useful archive, not proof that a competitor can recreate all those relationships.

Before cancelling:

1. Export the primary workspace and open the resulting file to confirm it contains the expected work.
2. Record any information from other workspaces separately.
3. Save recurring-task definitions and the planning preferences you will need to rebuild.
4. Select a small group of unfinished tasks to transfer or reconnect.
5. Confirm task duration, due dates and recurrence after import.
6. Retain the original export even if the new application cannot use every field.

Compare two kinds of continuity: can you plan tomorrow, and can you still explain last month’s work? A replacement may succeed at the first while requiring a separate archive for the second.

Run a normal week before adopting an annual subscription. Include a day with meetings, a day with interrupted focus time and a day when the plan is deliberately smaller. Judge whether you keep opening the new planner and understand where unfinished work went.

## Questions about replacing Sunsama

### Which option is closest to a simple daily board?

Ellie is the first candidate here for choosing a day from an unscheduled backlog. Its paid timeboxing features matter if you want tasks beside calendar events.

### Is Akiflow a cheaper alternative?

Not at the public monthly or annual-equivalent rates verified for this article. Consider it for capture and scheduling workflow differences.

### Can I keep Sunsama but stop automatic planning prompts?

Its account settings document controls for automated daily planning and shutdown prompts. Try those before assuming a new product is necessary.

### Does Sunsama export task data?

Yes. Its settings describe CSV and JSON exports from the primary workspace. That does not establish complete import compatibility with another product.

### Which alternative is free for ongoing use?

Ellie, Reclaim and TickTick have permanent free tiers, with different restrictions. None should be assumed to reproduce every Sunsama planning and review feature.

### Should I switch to obtain Apple calendar support?

Sunsama’s current FAQ already documents Apple account support. Investigate the particular issue you are having rather than using an outdated comparison as the reason to leave.

Try Ellie for a smaller daily board or Marvin for a configurable routine. Choose Reclaim for time protection and Motion for broader scheduling authority. Keep Sunsama when its deliberate review process is the part that helps you make realistic commitments.
