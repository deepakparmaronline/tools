# 7 Fiverr Alternatives for Buyers and Freelancers (2026)

There is no single best Fiverr alternative for every project. [Upwork](https://www.upwork.com/) is the broadest first comparison for buyers who need hourly or longer contracts, [Contra](https://contra.com/) suits independent creatives who want commission-free payments, and [Freelancer.com](https://www.freelancer.com/) fits buyers who want competing bids or design contests. If you are a freelancer, compare what you keep after fees and how each site helps you find clients; if you are hiring, compare the full checkout cost, work model and protection.

- **Upwork, Best for:** custom projects, hourly work and ongoing client relationships.
- **Contra, Best for:** portfolio-led creative work and freelancers who can bring their own clients.
- **Freelancer.com, Best for:** project bidding and design or naming contests.

## Why buyers and freelancers leave Fiverr

Fiverr is built around service listings called Gigs. A seller defines a service, scope, delivery time, revisions and price, then a buyer orders that package or requests a custom offer. The model is useful when the work can be described before the project begins. It can feel restrictive when a project needs discovery, weekly hours, a long contract or a detailed statement of work.

Fees are another reason to compare. Fiverr’s official Earnings page says freelancers receive 80% of the purchase amount for completed orders, including extras and tips. The buyer Help Center lists a standard 5.5% service fee and an extra US$3.50 on orders under US$200. The Payment Terms separately describe a US$5 Order Initiation Fee for eligible hourly contracts. Check the final checkout breakdown for your region instead of assuming one fee example covers every purchase.

Buyers may also need more control over proposal review, milestones, time tracking, specialist screening or team contracts. Freelancers may want a different client-discovery model, a portfolio that works beyond one marketplace, or fee rules that change with the contract or client relationship. These are reasons to compare workflows, not proof that Fiverr is poor for every user.

## 1. Upwork: best for custom and ongoing projects

**Best for:** clients who need a defined brief but want to discuss the scope with several freelancers, and freelancers who prefer proposals, hourly contracts or longer client relationships.

Upwork supports hourly and fixed-price work. Freelancers apply to jobs using Connects or receive invitations; clients review proposals and choose a person for a contract. The official pricing page says the Basic freelancer plan is free and standard freelancer service fees range from 0% to 15% per contract. The exact fee is shown before a proposal or offer is accepted and stays fixed once the contract begins. Freelancer Plus is listed at US$19.99 per month, with 100 Connects monthly; extra Connects cost US$0.15 each. [Upwork freelancer pricing, checked October 31, 2026.](https://www.upwork.com/pricing/freelancer)

**Pros:** hourly and fixed-price options; better fit for work that changes through milestones; clear per-contract fee disclosure.

**Cons:** proposal writing and Connects add work and possible cost; clients must compare bids and review fit rather than selecting a ready-made package.

**Who should skip it:** buyers who want a small, clearly packaged service with minimal briefing may prefer Fiverr’s direct Gig flow. Freelancers who dislike applying for projects may prefer a portfolio-led model.

## 2. Contra: best for portfolio-led creative work

**Best for:** designers, developers, marketers and other independents who want a portfolio, contracts, invoices and client payments in one workspace, especially when they can bring some of their own clients.

Contra describes freelancer payments as commission-free. Its official pricing page lists a free plan and Pro at US$29 per month or US$199 per year. The free plan charges the client US$15 or US$29 per payment depending on the amount; Contra Pro waives those platform fees. Payment processor fees can still apply depending on the method. [Contra pricing, checked October 31, 2026.](https://contra.com/pricing) [Contra fee details, checked October 31, 2026.](https://help.contra.com/en/articles/9322934-fees-overview)

**Pros:** no freelancer commission; portfolio and contract tools; supports invoices for work with clients who do not need to discover you through a marketplace.

**Cons:** a commission-free payment does not guarantee a steady flow of inbound leads; clients still need to account for platform and processor charges on the free plan.

**Who should skip it:** freelancers who depend on a large feed of ready-to-bid jobs may prefer Upwork or Freelancer.com. Buyers who want a fixed Gig catalog should check whether the provider’s portfolio and proposal match their brief.

## 3. Freelancer.com: best for bidding and contests

**Best for:** clients who want to publish a project and compare competing bids, or run a contest for a visual concept, name or similar deliverable.

Freelancer.com lets employers post projects and receive bids. Its official fee page says fixed-price employers pay 3% of the project value or US$3, whichever is greater; hourly employers pay 3% on each payment. Freelancers pay 10% or US$5, whichever is greater, on fixed-price projects and 10% on hourly payments. Contest rules and optional upgrades differ. [Freelancer.com fees, checked October 31, 2026.](https://www.freelancer.com/feesandcharges)

**Pros:** bidding creates several proposals to compare; contests suit work where seeing different concepts is useful; the published fee schedule separates buyer and freelancer costs.

**Cons:** a high bid count creates more screening work; contest entries are not the same as a collaborative brief and milestone workflow.

**Who should skip it:** buyers who want one selected specialist to scope a complex engagement may find proposal-based contract work easier to manage. Freelancers who do not want to compete on bid price should compare other models.

## 4. PeoplePerHour: best for offers and repeat clients

**Best for:** freelancers who want to publish fixed-scope offers and also respond to custom briefs, particularly when they expect to build repeat work with the same buyer.

PeoplePerHour combines packaged offers with project proposals. Its official help article lists freelancer service fees by lifetime billing with each buyer: 20% below £250, 7.5% between £250 and £5,000, and 3.5% above £5,000, excluding VAT. The article was last updated in December 2021, so confirm the current fee displayed in your account before pricing a project. [PeoplePerHour commission fees, checked October 31, 2026.](https://support.peopleperhour.com/hc/en-us/articles/205218337-Freelancer-commission-fees)

**Pros:** both fixed-scope offers and custom proposals; the published fee structure rewards repeat billing with a buyer.

**Cons:** the fee schedule is older than the other sources checked here; the lowest rate depends on the lifetime value of a particular client relationship.

**Who should skip it:** freelancers who need a fee table confirmed for the current year before quoting should get written confirmation first. Buyers looking for senior specialists through a formal vetting process may prefer Toptal.

## 5. Guru: best for flexible contract arrangements

**Best for:** freelancers and clients who want to choose among hourly, fixed-price, milestone or recurring work and keep project communication organized in a WorkRoom.

Guru’s official freelancer pricing page lists a free Basic membership with a 9% job fee and paid memberships with lower fees. The exact membership and bid allowance should be checked on the current pricing page before joining. [Guru freelancer pricing, checked October 31, 2026.](https://www.guru.com/pricing-freelancer/) The official job-fee guide explains that the fee depends on membership and that the employer may agree to cover part of it. [Guru job fee, checked October 31, 2026.](https://www.guru.com/help/freelancer/about-guru-freelancer/fees/job-fee)

**Pros:** several contract types; paid memberships can lower the published freelancer fee; useful when a project needs milestones or recurring invoices.

**Cons:** bid limits and membership costs can change the real cost; a paid plan is not automatically worthwhile if you send few quotes.

**Who should skip it:** freelancers who want a very simple service storefront may prefer Fiverr. Buyers who want a highly screened expert pool should compare Toptal.

## 6. Toptal: best for vetted specialists

**Best for:** businesses hiring experienced developers, designers, finance specialists or other experts for a more involved project.

Toptal is not an open catalog of small Gigs. It uses a screening and matching process. Its official explanation says fewer than 3% of applicants are typically accepted and the full screening process can take 3 to 8 weeks. This is Toptal’s own description of its process, not an independent audit. Pricing is quote-based; a standard public rate was not found on the official pages checked October 31, 2026. [Toptal screening process, checked October 31, 2026.](https://www.toptal.com/top-3-percent)

**Pros:** a curated pool; a better fit when senior expertise and matching matter more than a quick packaged order.

**Cons:** the screening process is slower than browsing a Gig; pricing is not presented as a simple public package table.

**Who should skip it:** buyers with a small task or strict low budget may find the process too heavy. Freelancers who want open marketplace discovery should expect a screening hurdle.

## 7. 99designs: best for design contests and branding

**Best for:** buyers who need logo, brand identity or another visual design and want to compare several concepts or hire a designer directly.

99designs focuses on design work rather than general writing, development, marketing and admin services. Its US logo-design pricing page lists contest packages starting at US$249, with Bronze at US$249, Silver at US$399, Gold at US$799 and Platinum at US$1,050 before sales tax. Other design categories use different pricing. [99designs logo pricing, checked October 31, 2026.](https://99designs.com/pricing) The platform also supports one-to-one projects. [99designs how it works, checked October 31, 2026.](https://99designs.com/how-it-works)

**Pros:** multiple concepts can help when the brief is visual; contest and one-to-one routes support different ways to commission design.

**Cons:** it is not a general Fiverr substitute for non-design work; a contest costs more than many small fixed-price tasks.

**Who should skip it:** businesses that need SEO, writing, coding or virtual-assistant work should choose a broader marketplace.

## What transfers when you leave Fiverr?

Fiverr’s Earnings page lets freelancers filter and download activity history, email a CSV activity report, and download a PDF statement for a full 12-month period. That is useful for bookkeeping, but it is not a complete migration of a freelance business. [Fiverr Earnings page, checked October 31, 2026.](https://help.fiverr.com/hc/en-us/articles/9234443621137-Your-earnings-page)

- Save your portfolio files, original work samples, service descriptions, packages, FAQs, client requirements and delivery templates outside the platform.
- Download available activity reports and financial statements, then check dates and amounts against your own records.
- Keep copies of contracts, invoices and tax records that you are allowed to retain. Store client files securely and follow any confidentiality terms.
- Do not assume reviews, account levels, search visibility, Gig URLs, order history or seller reputation can be imported into another marketplace. No official bulk-transfer route for these items was confirmed in the sources checked.
- Rebuild the new profile around the destination platform’s own format. A Gig description may need to become a portfolio case study, project proposal or fixed-scope offer.
- Tell active clients about a change only in a way that follows the platform’s current Terms of Service. Do not move an order or payment off-platform if the applicable rules prohibit it.

## How to choose without switching twice

- **Decide which side you are on.** Buyers should compare checkout cost and project protection. Freelancers should calculate net earnings and how they will find work.
- **Match the work model.** Use Fiverr or PeoplePerHour for packaged offers, Upwork for proposals and contracts, Freelancer.com for bids and contests, and Toptal for vetted specialists.
- **Calculate total cost.** Include platform fees, paid membership, Connects, payment processing, withdrawal charges and currency conversion where applicable.
- **Check the payment process.** Read how milestones, delivery approval, cancellation, refunds and disputes work before moving a high-value project.
- **Run a small pilot.** Start with one project and compare communication, scope changes, quality checks and payout timing before moving all work.
- **Keep your own records.** A marketplace profile is not a full business archive. Save deliverables and permitted records outside the account.

## Frequently asked questions

### Which Fiverr alternative has the lowest freelancer commission?

Contra advertises commission-free freelancer payments, but free-plan client platform fees and payment processor charges can still apply. Compare the complete fee path, not just the freelancer commission.

### Is Upwork better than Fiverr?

Upwork is a closer fit for proposals, hourly contracts and ongoing projects. Fiverr is simpler when the service can be purchased as a clearly scoped Gig. The better choice depends on the project and how each side pays.

### Can I move Fiverr reviews to another platform?

No official bulk migration route for Fiverr reviews or seller levels was found in the sources checked on October 31, 2026. Keep permitted records and build a new profile using the destination platform’s own rules.

### Is Fiverr free to use?

Creating a basic Gig can start without a monthly marketplace subscription, but sellers keep 80% of the purchase amount and buyers may pay service fees. Optional seller programs, payout methods and taxes can add cost.

### Which alternative is best for design contests?

Freelancer.com offers contests across several categories, while 99designs focuses on design and branding. Compare the brief format, contest rules, rights and refund terms before funding a project.
