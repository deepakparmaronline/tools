# 6 Freedom Alternatives for Website and App Blocking

Cold Turkey Blocker is a useful Freedom alternative for strict Windows or Mac sessions without a recurring Pro subscription. FocusMe suits configurable plans, while SelfControl is a free Mac option for timed website blocking. Consider Opal for phone-focused rules, one sec for a pause before opening distracting apps, or Apple Screen Time for built-in limits. These tools do not all replace Freedom's coordinated multi-device workflow. Freedom itself now has a real free tier, so check whether that already meets your needs. Choose by your devices, the interruption you want, and what must remain accessible during a session.

## What Freedom already does, including its free tier

[Freedom](https://freedom.to/) blocks distracting apps, websites, and internet access across supported devices. Its central appeal is coordinating a focus session rather than configuring an unrelated timer on every screen. If your distraction moves from a laptop to a phone, that coordination can be the feature worth keeping.

The current [Free and Premium guide](https://support.freedom.to/en/articles/13764747-what-s-included-in-free-and-premium-plans) lists a genuine free account tier. It includes Start Now sessions, custom blocklists, and unlimited devices. Free sessions cannot exceed two hours; recurring sessions, Start Later, and Locked Mode require Premium. This matters because older comparison pages describe Freedom as only a limited trial.

The [pricing page](https://freedom.to/premium) lists Premium at US$8.99 month to month and shows US$3.33 per month under the yearly choice. The latter is an annual-plan equivalent, not a US$3.33 rolling monthly subscription; confirm the annual charge at checkout. The page also advertises a discounted US$99.50 lifetime offer at the time checked. Offers, local taxes, and checkout totals can change.

Keep Freedom if the free limits are enough or if its coordinated paid schedule already solves the problem. Switch for a specific reason: a desktop-only one-time purchase, a more detailed plan, a different phone interaction, or an included operating-system feature. There is no need to replace it merely because another list calls something a productivity winner.

## Locking is a setting, not a universal guarantee

Freedom's [Locked Mode guide](https://support.freedom.to/en/articles/1802927-locked-mode) describes settings on the dashboard and on devices separately. The dashboard limits End Session to once in seven days when locked, and inactive upcoming sessions remain editable. On iOS, the guide explains that protecting Freedom's Screen Time permission requires a separate Screen Time passcode. These details are more useful than saying a session is impossible to escape.

Different alternatives make different tradeoffs between a timer, password, waiting period, and permission protection. Choose the level that fits your work and test it on a short session before committing to a long one. Required communications, authentication, and work resources should be accounted for in the blocklist you choose.

## Compare six different approaches

This comparison uses official product pages, plan documentation, and support guides checked October 8, 2026. It is research-based. We did not attempt bypasses, install device-management profiles, or measure changes in productivity. Suggested routines below are examples for evaluating a tool, not tested outcomes.

| Alternative | Main use case | Cost or limitation to check |
|---|---|---|
| Cold Turkey Blocker | Locked desktop website/app sessions | Free basics; Pro one-time purchase; Windows/Mac only |
| FocusMe | Plans with time and launch limits | Paid plans after trial; device and sync limits |
| SelfControl | Timed website blocking on a Mac | Free; timer remains active after restarting or deleting the app |
| Opal | Phone-focused schedules and app rules | Free tier; paid Hard Mode; current rule-limit documentation conflicts |
| one sec | Interrupting automatic app openings | Free one-app intervention; Pro for broader features |
| Apple Screen Time | Built-in Apple-device schedules and limits | Included; controls depend on OS version |

## Cold Turkey Blocker: consider a desktop-only commitment

[Cold Turkey Blocker](https://getcoldturkey.com/) targets website and app distractions on desktop computers. Its [pricing guide](https://getcoldturkey.com/pricing/) distinguishes Free timed website blocks from Pro features such as app blocking, schedules, all locking options, and breaks or allowances. Pro is a one-time purchase with lifetime updates. The fetched official page did not expose a reliable numeric purchase amount, so check its current checkout rather than relying on an old US$39 quotation.

The same guide confirms Windows and macOS support and a license for computers you personally use. It does not support Linux, ChromeOS, Android, or iOS. A desktop license therefore cannot replace Freedom's phone coverage on its own.

Its [feature documentation](https://getcoldturkey.com/features/) explains several locks, including a timer, schedule, password, and delay. These are not identical commitments. For example, someone who wants a daily desktop work period can investigate scheduling and allowances instead of creating a long continuous block.

Choose Cold Turkey when your distraction mainly lives on a Windows PC or Mac and a one-time Pro purchase fits. Skip it as a full multi-device replacement if you immediately move to a phone when blocked. Before locking, check required websites and supported browser setup. The detailed controls are the reason to consider it, not evidence that every possible workaround has been eliminated.

## FocusMe: build a plan around how you use apps

[FocusMe](https://focusme.com/how-it-works/) offers configurable blocking plans, password protection, and stricter modes. Its [FAQ](https://focusme.com/faq/) describes time limits, launch counts, and plans that operate at different times. That makes it relevant when you want to distinguish limited access from complete blocking rather than use the same rule all day.

For example, you might want a short allowance for a news site at lunch and a full block during a writing period. That is a proposed configuration to investigate, not a claim about a measured productivity improvement.

The [pricing page](https://focusme.com/pricing/) offers a 14-day trial without a credit card, recurring plans, and a US$249 lifetime option. The fetched page did not clearly expose the recurring checkout totals. Confirm those before comparing costs. A trial is not a permanent free desktop plan.

Coverage and sync need separate checks. The FAQ lists five desktop computers and two mobile devices per license. It documents Premium plan sync between Windows, Mac, and iPhone, while Android sync is still in development. Android availability does not mean identical synchronization.

Choose FocusMe for its plan controls if they match your device mix. Skip it as an assumed unlimited-device equivalent to Freedom. Review its account and activity settings too: the FAQ distinguishes local use, signed-in syncing, and optional Coach data, rather than describing every configuration as entirely offline.

## SelfControl: a free Mac timer with a firm consequence

[SelfControl](https://selfcontrolapp.com/) is a free, open-source macOS app for restricting access to selected websites, mail servers, and other internet destinations. You choose a duration and a blocklist. Its official page says the block persists until the timer expires, including after a restart or deletion of the app.

This is a useful option when you want a straightforward website-blocking session on one Mac and do not need a subscription, account-based coordination, or phone controls. It is a partial Freedom substitute, focused on internet destinations rather than a full cross-device app-management workflow.

The main advantage and main tradeoff are the same: the timer's commitment. A writer may like having a distracting site unavailable for a chosen period, but adding a required mail server or sign-in resource to that list can interrupt legitimate work. Review the list before starting, and begin with a short, narrow session.

Choose SelfControl for Mac-only timed website blocking. Skip it when your routine requires easy mid-session edits, detailed app allowances, or coordination with Android or Windows. Free software can still impose a significant workflow cost if the configuration is wrong. This recommendation is based on its documented behavior; we did not personally test a block or attempt to cancel one.

## Opal: phone rules with platform-specific qualifications

[Opal](https://opalapp.com/) offers apps for iPhone, Mac, and Android, with schedules, time limits, and open limits. Its [pricing page](https://opalapp.com/pricing) lists a free plan and Pro at US$99.99 billed annually, alongside a US$399 lifetime purchase. The annual bill is the meaningful price, rather than treating its displayed US$8.29 monthly equivalent as month-to-month access.

There are two official-page conflicts to note. The pricing page says one free Rule, while the [Schedules help guide](https://help.opalapp.com/article/how-to-use-schedules) says three. A separate monthly-looking card carries an annual-billing label. We therefore do not promise a universal free-rule allowance or a verified rolling monthly price. Check the current app and checkout.

The iOS help guide explains paid Hard Mode and a once-weekly Emergency Pass. Its [blocking troubleshooting guide](https://help.opalapp.com/article/why-isnt-blocking-working-correctly) also notes limits in third-party browsers and platform restrictions on selected apps/sites. Available apps do not establish equal behavior everywhere.

Choose Opal when your main goal is configuring phone app rules and you accept the verified plan and platform boundaries. Skip it as a guaranteed Windows replacement or as universally impossible to disable. Check your particular browser, OS, and emergency-access behavior before scheduling a long block. The interface and analytics may appeal, but this article does not adopt vendor time-saved or wellbeing statistics.

## one sec: interrupt the opening habit, with optional blocking

[one sec](https://one-sec.app/) focuses on interventions before a distracting app or website opens. A breathing prompt or other pause asks you to decide whether to continue. That is a different first-line approach from making a whole multi-device work session unavailable.

Its [Pro guide](https://tutorials.one-sec.app/en/articles/3036418) says the free version targets one app and Pro expands to multiple apps and additional features. The [FAQ](https://one-sec.app/faq/) lists regional example prices of €3.99 monthly, €14.99 annually, or €99.99 lifetime, explicitly noting that prices vary by region. Confirm your own store or checkout, including renewal and trial terms.

Do not reduce one sec to a pause-only product. Its current FAQ also describes complete blocks and scheduled blocking. It offers iOS and Android apps and a browser extension, but acknowledges platform differences. One account across devices is not evidence that every session behaves like Freedom's coordinated blocks.

Choose one sec when you want to interrupt an automatic opening and assess whether that interaction fits you. Skip it as an assumed substitute for locked desktop app sessions without verifying those features on the required platform. We do not turn its vendor-cited research into a promised personal result; the comparison here concerns documented software behavior and plan scope.

## Apple Screen Time: check the included option first

Apple's [Screen Time setup guide](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/get-started-with-screen-time-iphbfa595995/ios) documents built-in controls for your own device. There is no separate app subscription. This makes it a worthwhile first candidate when the goal is a simple limit on an Apple device rather than a coordinated Windows-and-Android workflow.

Use documentation for your OS version. Apple's current [Schedules and Time Allowances guide](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/set-schedules-with-screen-time-iphb0c7313c9/ios) requires version 27 or later across participating Apple devices for those newer features. Older versions use different controls and labels. Do not follow a screenshot from one version and assume your phone has the same options.

The included option can help you establish whether ordinary schedules and allowances are enough before paying for another app. It also avoids moving a small requirement into a new vendor account. However, inclusion does not mean the controls match Freedom's sessions, lock behavior, or supported platforms.

Choose Screen Time when Apple-device limits meet the requirement. Skip it as a direct replacement for coordinating a Windows PC and Android phone. Review allowed apps and your own ability to change settings. If ignoring a reminder is the problem, a reminder-only configuration will not become a firm block simply because a time limit was set.

## Pick by where the distraction goes next

For a desktop-only routine, trial Cold Turkey or FocusMe. SelfControl is the simpler Mac website option if its timer commitment suits you. If the distraction mainly occurs when opening phone apps, compare Opal's rules with one sec's interventions. For a basic Apple-device allowance, inspect Screen Time first.

If you move to another device immediately after a block, keep Freedom in the comparison. Two separate tools can cover two devices without starting and ending together. Treat that manual coordination as a cost, not a feature you have verified merely by installing both.

Also decide what should remain accessible. Blocking an entire platform may conflict with a necessary work message or course video on that same service. Investigate supported exceptions or a narrower rule. A blocker is most useful when its configuration protects a particular task without blocking the tools that task requires.

## Rebuild the schedule before cancelling the old plan

Record your Freedom lists, recurring sessions, timezone, exceptions, and participating devices. Review what each alternative can actually represent. This research did not establish a universal Freedom-to-alternative import format, so budget for manual rebuilding rather than promising a one-click migration.

Create one short trial session. Check the distracting site or app, then check required email, two-factor authentication, a work document, and any service sharing the same domain. On the phone, check the app and its website version where relevant. This is your proposed acceptance checklist, not a test we conducted.

Avoid overlapping strict blockers during the trial. If two schedules restrict the same resource, troubleshooting becomes harder. Disable or retire the old schedule at a planned time, after the new configuration has passed the checklist. Review whether uninstalling is prevented during an active lock before trying to remove the old software.

Subscriptions need their own action. Removing an app does not cancel a vendor or app-store renewal. Check the billing provider, renewal date, and access period before cancelling. Keep a record of the settings you need, but do not assume usage statistics and saved plans are transferable between products.

## Questions before switching

### Is there a genuinely free Freedom alternative?

SelfControl is free for its Mac website-blocking scope, and other products offer limited free features. Freedom itself now has a free account tier. Compare the limits and device coverage rather than calling every free download a full replacement.

### Which option avoids a recurring desktop subscription?

Cold Turkey Pro uses a one-time purchase, while SelfControl is free for a narrower Mac workflow. FocusMe also advertises a lifetime option. Check the current license terms and the features you require before comparing total costs.

### Is one sec only a breathing pause?

No. Its current documentation also describes full and scheduled blocks. The opening intervention remains a useful reason to consider it, but capabilities and plan access should be checked for the platform you use.

### Does any tool block everything on every device?

That is too broad a promise. Platform permissions, supported browsers, plan limits, and settings matter. Even a product with phone and desktop apps may not sync all plans or support identical blocks on both.

### Will my Freedom lists and schedule import automatically?

No universal transfer was verified for this shortlist. Record the lists, exceptions, times, and devices, then recreate one session and check it before moving the rest. Cancelling billing is separate from transferring the setup.

## Start with one short session

Choose Cold Turkey for a desktop commitment, FocusMe for configurable plans, or SelfControl for free timed Mac website blocks. Investigate Opal and one sec for phone behavior, and Screen Time for included Apple controls. Keep Freedom when its coordinated sessions or free tier already fit. The next step is a small trial using your actual distraction and the work resources that must stay available.
