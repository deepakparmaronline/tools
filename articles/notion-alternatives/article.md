# 7 Notion Alternatives for Docs, Wikis and Projects in 2026

**Short answer:** The best Notion alternative depends on the part of Notion you want to replace. ClickUp is a strong fit for task-first teams, Slab is a focused company wiki, and Obsidian is better for local Markdown notes. Confluence, Nuclino, Anytype and Taskade cover other needs, but none copies every Notion feature.

## Why teams look for a Notion alternative

Notion combines documents, wikis, databases and projects, so many teams start there. The reasons to compare another tool usually appear after the first workspace has grown. A team may want stronger task controls, a smaller and simpler wiki, a local copy of every note, more specific security controls or a different way to budget AI use.

- **Project delivery:** Notion can track tasks, but a project team may need time tracking, workload, sprint reports and more detailed task hierarchy.
- **Knowledge publishing:** a company that mainly needs approved internal articles may prefer a focused wiki with fewer database choices.
- **Offline and ownership:** users who want plain local files may prefer Obsidian. Anytype is another local-first option with a different sync model.
- **Workspace complexity:** a flexible tool can become hard to maintain if every team creates different databases and status rules.
- **Budget:** per-member plans, custom domains and Custom Agent credits should be compared with the work actually used.

## 1. ClickUp: best for task-first project teams

ClickUp is a better choice when the main job is assigning and tracking work. Tasks, lists, folders and spaces give projects a clear structure. The product also has Docs, goals, dashboards, Gantt views, time tracking and sprint features. A team can keep a brief beside the task list and report on delivery without building every workflow from scratch.

ClickUp's Free Forever plan is a useful starting point. Its official pricing FAQ lists Unlimited at US$7 per user/month and Business at US$12 per user/month when billed yearly. The current pricing page also presents AI options separately, so check whether ClickUp Brain or another AI add-on is required. ClickUp is a strong pick for project managers, agencies and teams that need task ownership. It is less attractive if the main need is a calm, wiki-first writing space with little project administration.

**Choose it when:** deadlines, owners, dependencies, dashboards and delivery reports matter more than a flexible document tree. **Skip it when:** most work is long-form reference material and the team does not need a full task system.

## 2. Confluence: best for enterprise knowledge and Jira teams

Confluence is built around spaces and pages for team knowledge. It fits organizations that already use Jira or other Atlassian products and want a shared place for project documentation, meeting notes, decisions and internal guidance. Atlassian also offers whiteboards, databases and AI features across its product set, but the exact feature set depends on plan and connected products.

Confluence has a Free plan for up to 10 users. The Atlassian pricing page shows Standard from about US$5.42 per user/month in its displayed calculator, but the final amount depends on team size and billing mode. Its value is strongest when a company already has Atlassian administration and project workflows. A solo writer may find the space and permission model heavier than needed.

**Choose it when:** the wiki must connect to Jira projects, team spaces and organization controls. **Skip it when:** you mainly want a personal notebook or a lightweight database for flexible work.

## 3. Slab: best for a focused company wiki

Slab focuses on team knowledge rather than trying to become every team's project database. It gives a company a place for posts, topics and shared reference material. Its search connects knowledge from Slab and supported external sources, which helps when information is spread across several work apps. The focused model can make it easier to explain where a policy or process belongs.

Slab's public pricing lists Free for up to 10 users, Startup at US$6.67 per user/month and Business at US$12.50 per user/month when billed annually. Check the current plan page for feature and user limits. Slab is a strong choice for a team that wants a maintained wiki. It is not a direct replacement for Notion's flexible databases, project views or custom internal tools.

**Choose it when:** the main job is writing, finding and maintaining team knowledge. **Skip it when:** you need complex project records, task dependencies or many custom database views.

## 4. Nuclino: best for a lightweight team workspace

Nuclino combines collaborative documents, a team wiki and simple project views. The interface is designed to stay light, and teams can connect pages, use canvases and arrange information in list, board, table or graph views. Its Free plan allows up to 50 items, up to three canvases and 2 GB of total storage. The vendor's solutions pages list advanced plans starting from US$6 per user/month.

Nuclino is a good fit when Notion feels too open-ended. A small team can create pages, link related notes and keep a simple knowledge base without building a large system of databases. Business adds features such as AI, audit logs, team insights, SSO and a custom publishing domain. The trade-off is fewer deep database and customization options than Notion.

**Choose it when:** you want a quick team wiki with simple collaboration. **Skip it when:** your workflow depends on complex relations, rollups or highly customized project databases.

## 5. Obsidian: best for local Markdown knowledge

Obsidian stores notes as Markdown files in a local vault. The core app is free, does not require an account and lets you keep files on your own device. Backlinks, graph view and community plugins help users connect notes and adapt the editor. This makes it useful for research, personal knowledge management and people who prefer files they can open with other tools.

Obsidian Sync is listed at US$4 per user/month when billed annually or US$5 monthly. Publish is US$8 per site/month annually or US$10 monthly. The optional commercial license is US$50 per user/year, but Obsidian says it is not required for commercial use. Sync adds encrypted syncing and shared vaults. Obsidian is not a ready-made team wiki or project manager, so collaboration and structured records need plugins or a separate system.

**Choose it when:** local files, offline access and long-term ownership matter most. **Skip it when:** your team needs managed permissions, live databases and a shared workspace with little setup.

## 6. Anytype: best for local-first private knowledge

Anytype is a local-first workspace built around objects, relations and shared spaces. It is aimed at people who want more control over their information and a different model from a cloud-first wiki. The product supports personal knowledge, projects and collaboration, with a focus on private data and peer-to-peer sync. Check its current documentation for the exact behavior of shared spaces and network sync before using it for a team that needs central administration.

The public pricing page lists Free with 100 MB of remote storage, Plus at US$4/month, Pro at US$8/month and Ultra at US$16/month. Those prices are displayed per plan rather than as a standard per-seat team quote. Anytype also has a separate Business offering with admin and SSO features. It is worth comparing when privacy and local-first design matter more than a conventional cloud workspace.

**Choose it when:** private knowledge and local-first data are priorities. **Skip it when:** you need a familiar SaaS admin model or a large set of standard business integrations.

## 7. Taskade: best for AI agents and app workflows

Taskade combines projects, AI agents, automations and AI app building. It can be a Notion alternative when a team wants documents and project information to feed agents or workflows. The product is broader than a wiki. Its value is in building small apps and connecting workspace data to AI actions, not in copying every Notion database view.

Taskade's pricing page shows a Free plan for one user, Starter at US$6/month with three users and 10,000 monthly AI credits, Pro at US$16/month with 10 users and 50,000 credits, and Business at US$40/month with unlimited users and 150,000 credits in the displayed view. The page includes a monthly/yearly selector, so confirm the billing mode before checkout. Read the [Taskade review](/reviews/taskade-review/) and its [Taskade alternatives guide](/alternatives/taskade-alternatives/) if AI app workflows are part of your shortlist.

**Choose it when:** you want projects, agents and app workflows in one place. **Skip it when:** you mainly need a traditional company wiki or a clean personal notes app.

## How to choose the right alternative

Write down the three workflows you use most in Notion. For example, a company may use a wiki, a product roadmap and a task board. Decide which one causes the most friction. If the problem is task ownership, test ClickUp. If the problem is finding approved knowledge, test Slab or Confluence. If the problem is personal note ownership, test Obsidian or Anytype. If the problem is a simple team wiki, test Nuclino.

Then compare the real cost. Count billable seats, guests, storage, AI credits, custom domains and integrations. A free plan may have an item limit, a seat limit or a local-only model. A lower monthly price does not help if the team has to buy another product to replace the feature it loses. Use the same sample workflow in each tool and record the setup time, permission steps and reporting work.

## How to move from Notion without losing important work

- List the pages and databases that people still use. Archive old projects before exporting.
- Export a small workspace sample as Markdown, HTML and CSV. Keep attachments with the export.
- Check nested pages, links, images, database properties and dates. Do not assume a CSV contains formulas or relations.
- Import the sample into the new tool. Taskade documents a Markdown import path for Notion exports, while Obsidian can work directly with Markdown files.
- Rebuild the parts that do not transfer as native objects: relations, rollups, views, automations, permissions, formulas and dashboards.
- Give users a short period to review the new workspace. Keep the original Notion workspace available until the team signs off.
- Choose one system as the source of truth for each type of record. Avoid updating the same task in two tools.

Notion's export guide says workspace exports can include HTML, Markdown and CSV plus uploaded files, but private pages that the exporter cannot access and content excluded by teamspace settings may be missing. It also says an export cannot instantly recreate the original workspace. Treat migration as a controlled project, not a file upload.

## Common questions

### Which Notion alternative is best for a team?

ClickUp is a strong choice when task management is the main need. Slab is better for a focused company wiki, while Confluence suits organizations that already rely on Jira and Atlassian administration.

### Which Notion alternative has a free plan?

ClickUp, Confluence, Slab, Nuclino, Obsidian, Anytype and Taskade all publish free entry options. Their limits differ, so compare seats, items, storage, local features and paid add-ons rather than treating every free plan as equal.

### Which Notion alternative works offline?

Obsidian stores notes as local Markdown files. Anytype is built around local-first data and sync. Notion also supports downloaded pages in its desktop and mobile apps, but it does not provide the same full local-file workflow.

### Can I move my Notion workspace to another tool?

Notion can export pages as HTML or Markdown and databases as CSV. You should still expect to rebuild relations, formulas, views, permissions and automations that do not transfer as native objects.

### Which alternative is closest to Notion?

Nuclino is a close option for a lighter team wiki and collaborative docs. Taskade is closer when a team wants docs, projects and AI agents together. Neither reproduces every Notion database and permission feature.
