# Help Scout alternatives

Canonical: https://toolboxkart.tech/helpscout/alternatives/

**Short answer:** Front is the closest Help Scout alternative for teams that want a shared inbox and collaborative customer conversations. Freshdesk is a stronger first comparison for structured ticketing and a lower paid entry price. Zendesk suits teams with more complex routing and broader service operations. The other choices fit chat-first SaaS, Zoho users, ecommerce support or team email.

This guide compares seven Help Scout alternatives by product model, public pricing, free access, AI charges and migration detail. Help Scout itself remains a good fit when shared conversations and Docs are enough. A switch makes sense when a specific limit affects cost, routing, channels, reporting or data portability. The facts below were checked against official vendor pages on November 11, 2026. This is documentation-based research, not a hands-on test.

- **Front. Best closest replacement:** collaborative shared inboxes, team discussions and several customer channels.
- **Freshdesk. Best for structured ticketing:** a help desk with ticket fields, portal, knowledge base and reporting.
- **Zendesk. Best for complex support operations:** routing, customer context, automation and a broader suite.

## Why teams look beyond Help Scout

Help Scout is not automatically too simple for a growing team. Its paid plans include workflows, SLAs, customer portal options and AI assistance. The reason to compare alternatives is more specific. The Free plan has a 100-contact monthly limit and only one inbox and one Docs site. Paid plans use per-user pricing. Envoy is priced by resolution. The cost can rise when more agents, inboxes or AI resolutions are added.

Data migration is another reason to compare. Help Scout's official import guide brings selected email or ticket history, customer profiles and tags from supported systems. It does not recreate every old user, inbox setting, knowledge article, saved reply, macro, rule, automation, custom field or custom status. If you need a full copy of a complex help desk, you must plan the parts that need to be rebuilt.

Some teams need a more formal ticket model with custom fields, groups and priority rules. Others want an ecommerce help desk that sees orders and returns, or a chat-first platform with an AI agent. The right replacement is the one that fixes the actual workflow problem without adding a larger admin burden.

## 1. Front: the closest shared-inbox alternative

Front is the first product to compare when the reason for leaving Help Scout is team collaboration around shared customer messages. It combines shared inboxes with internal comments, assignments, rules, analytics and connections to other business tools. The model feels closer to a collaborative email workspace than a traditional ticket queue. It can suit customer operations teams that work across email and other channels and need a shared view of the customer.

Front's public pricing page lists Starter at US$25, Professional at US$65 and Enterprise at US$105 per seat/month when billed annually. Starter is capped at 10 seats and Professional at 50 seats on the published page. Enterprise has more advanced controls and sales-led terms. There is a free trial, but the public page does not list a permanent free plan. Confirm the channel and AI add-ons that your team will use.

**Why choose it:** Front is a close fit when several teammates need to discuss and own the same customer thread. Its Help Scout importer can bring inboxes, teammates, tags, contacts and messages. The official help page says notes and comments are not imported, and the importer is not actively maintained. Front points teams toward migration partners for a more complete move. Test a sample and confirm the migration scope before switching.

**Trade-off:** Front is seat-based and can become expensive as the team grows. Some advanced features and channels depend on plan. **Skip it if:** your main need is a low-cost ticket queue or a simple knowledge base rather than team collaboration around messages.

## 2. Freshdesk: structured ticketing at a lower entry price

Freshdesk is worth comparing when a shared conversation model no longer gives managers enough control over ticket fields, groups, priorities or reporting. The product includes ticketing, a shared inbox, customer portal, knowledge base and analytics. Freshworks describes Freshdesk as the email-first help desk; Freshdesk Omni is the separate product to review when you need a broader channel bundle.

The official pricing page checked November 11, 2026 lists Growth at US$19, Pro at US$55 and Enterprise at US$89 per agent/month billed annually. The public page lists a 14-day trial and does not show a permanent Free plan. Freshworks says new customers on Growth, Pro or Enterprise receive a one-time complimentary 500-session Freddy AI Agent allowance. Extra session packs cost US$49 per 100 for the current billing cycle. Freddy AI Copilot is listed at US$29 per agent/month on Pro and Enterprise as an add-on.

**Why choose it:** Freshdesk gives a support manager a clearer ticket structure and customer portal. It is a useful first comparison when Help Scout's conversation model is too light for the team's process. **Trade-off:** the AI allowance is a one-time grant, not a monthly reset, and Freshdesk Omni should not be assumed to be included in the email-first price. Read the [Freshdesk guide](/freshdesk/), [Freshdesk review](/freshdesk/review/) and [Freshdesk vs Help Scout comparison](/compare/freshdesk-vs-help-scout/).

## 3. Zendesk: for complex support operations

Zendesk is a broad support suite for teams that need ticket routing, customer context, analytics, automation and several service channels. The pricing page lists Support Team at US$19, Suite Team at US$55 and Suite Professional at US$115 per agent/month when paid yearly. The monthly equivalents shown are higher. Suite plans add messaging, live chat, knowledge and other controls that are not part of the Support Team entry plan.

**Why choose it:** Zendesk is worth a shortlist when several teams share the support queue, routing rules matter and managers need more control over service operations. The official migration guidance describes API-based paths for tickets, users, organizations and help centre content, but some ticket metrics are not imported by the documented process. **Trade-off:** the plan you need may cost more than the entry price, and a migration still needs field mapping. Read the [Zendesk guide](/zendesk/) and [Zendesk review](/zendesk/review/).

## 4. Intercom: for chat-first SaaS and AI support

Intercom combines a shared inbox and ticketing with Messenger, a public help centre, workflows and Fin AI Agent. It fits a software company that wants customer conversations to start in the product or on the website. The official pricing page currently shows Essential at US$19 per seat/month for new customers, beside a US$29 regular price. Advanced is US$85 and Expert US$132 per seat/month. Fin starts at US$0.99 per outcome. The outcome charge is separate from seats and is counted once per conversation under the vendor's definition.

**Why choose it:** Intercom is a better comparison when live chat, in-app support and AI resolution are central to the product experience. **Trade-off:** the discounted Essential price is a promotion, and usage-based charges for Fin, SMS, WhatsApp and other channels can change the total. Compare the regular price and your expected Fin outcomes, not only the promotional seat rate.

## 5. Zoho Desk: for small teams and Zoho users

Zoho Desk is a ticketing help desk with a free edition for up to three agents. It is worth checking when a small team wants to start without a per-seat bill or when the business already uses Zoho CRM and related apps. The paid plan table is localized by region and billing. The official page view checked for this guide did not show one universal US price, so this article does not invent a paid starting amount.

**Why choose it:** Zoho Desk can make sense when the team needs ticketing and a help centre and already works in the Zoho ecosystem. **Trade-off:** compare the exact regional plan, department limits, automation, reporting and AI access. A free tier for three agents may be enough for a very small queue, but it is not the same as a five-user Help Scout plan with Docs and a 100-contact limit.

## 6. Gorgias: for ecommerce support

Gorgias is built for ecommerce customer service. Its product pages focus on support conversations that use store, order and customer context. That can be a better fit than a general shared inbox when agents spend much of the day answering questions about shipping, returns, orders and subscriptions.

Gorgias prices by ticket volume rather than a simple per-agent seat rate. The official pricing page shows ticket bands from small monthly volumes through larger plans and describes AI Agent charges by resolved conversation. The page view checked for this guide did not expose a stable dollar amount for each band, so the price is left out. Use the vendor's calculator or request a quote with your expected ticket volume.

**Why choose it:** ecommerce order context is central to the workflow. **Trade-off:** a ticket-volume model is not directly comparable with Help Scout's per-user price and Envoy resolution charge. It may be too specialized if the business is not ecommerce-led.

## 7. Missive: for team email and internal collaboration

Missive is a collaborative email and messaging workspace. It gives teams shared accounts, conversations, tasks, internal discussions, rules and integrations. It can suit a small team that wants to work together in email and social or SMS conversations without adopting a full help desk.

The official pricing page lists Starter at US$14, Productive at US$24 and Business at US$36 per user/month when billed yearly. A 30-day free trial is offered. Rules, automation, analytics and API access appear on higher plans. **Why choose it:** it can be a simpler and lower-cost fit when the work is mostly shared email and internal collaboration. **Trade-off:** it is not a direct substitute for a help desk with the same ticket portal, knowledge base and service controls. Confirm that the team's support workflow does not depend on those features.

## Plan the move before changing email forwarding

Help Scout's official import guide lists supported sources such as Freshdesk, Zendesk, Intercom and Zoho Desk. The import is available to Account Owners and Administrators on Standard, Plus and Pro. It can bring selected emails or tickets with customer profiles and tags. The guide says users and inbox settings, knowledge base articles, saved replies, macros, ticket rules, automations, custom fields and custom statuses are not imported as native Help Scout objects.

If you are leaving Help Scout, check the destination's importer rather than assuming the reverse path has the same coverage. Front's official guide lists inboxes, teammates, tags, contacts and messages, but not notes or comments, and it recommends migration partners because the importer is not actively maintained. Zendesk has API-based import options, but its documentation notes limits on ticket metrics. Freshdesk ticket-list export is useful for filtered data, not proof of a complete account archive.

- List the objects you need: conversations, replies, notes, attachments, customers, tags, custom fields, macros, workflows, knowledge articles and reports.
- Ask the destination vendor which objects it imports and which must be rebuilt.
- Export a small sample with older, reopened and attachment-heavy cases.
- Compare record counts, timestamps, customer profiles and attachments.
- Run the new inbox with a small group before changing the public support address.
- Keep the source system and a rollback plan until the team has checked the new workflow.

## How to choose the right Help Scout alternative

- **Start with the work model.** Choose a shared inbox for collaborative email, ticketing for structured queues, chat-first support for in-product conversations and an ecommerce tool for order-heavy questions.
- **Price the whole system.** Include seats, ticket volume, AI resolutions, messaging, Docs sites, integrations, annual commitment and implementation.
- **Check the free option.** A free plan may cap contacts, agents, storage or channels. A free trial is not a permanent free plan.
- **Test migration.** Verify notes, attachments, customer records, tags and automations. Do not assume an import tool moves every object.
- **Keep the first rollout small.** Test a normal request, an escalation, a reopened case and an attachment before switching all support traffic.

## Common questions

### What is the closest alternative to Help Scout?

Front is the closest shared-inbox comparison because it focuses on collaborative customer messages. Freshdesk is a better first comparison when the team wants structured ticketing and a customer portal. The right choice depends on the workflow, not only the brand.

### Is there a free Help Scout alternative?

Zoho Desk lists a free edition for up to three agents. Several products offer trials rather than permanent free plans. Check whether the free tier includes the inbox, channels and customer history your team needs.

### Can I move Help Scout data to Front?

Front documents a Help Scout importer for inboxes, teammates, tags, contacts and messages. It does not import notes or comments, and the importer is not actively maintained. Front recommends migration partners for a more complete move.

### Does Help Scout import tickets from other help desks?

Yes. Its official import guide lists several supported sources, including Freshdesk and Zendesk. It does not import every user setting, knowledge article, saved reply, automation or custom field.

### Is Intercom cheaper than Help Scout?

Not by seat price alone. Intercom shows a new-customer Essential promotion and charges separately for Fin outcomes and some channels. Help Scout charges per user and lists Envoy by resolution. Compare the full seat count and usage estimate.
