# 6 Arc Search Alternatives for Mobile Browsing and AI Answers

Brave and DuckDuckGo Browser are practical Arc Search alternatives if your priority is cleaner mobile browsing with built-in protections. Firefox suits cross-device browsing and compatible Android extensions; Safari is worth trying first on an iPhone. Microsoft Edge is a candidate for AI assistance inside a mobile browser. Perplexity's mobile app is closer to the answer-search part of Browse for Me, but is only a partial browser replacement. Keep Arc Search if its quick searches and reading workflow still fit. Choose what you need to replace first: the browser, the AI answers, or the connection to desktop Arc.

## Arc Search is a mobile browser, not desktop Arc

[Arc Search](https://arc.net/search) combines regular mobile browsing with Browse for Me, built-in blocking, reader mode, and inactive-tab archiving. The product is free to download. Its [mobile documentation](https://resources.arc.net/hc/en-us/articles/20887042551831-Arc-for-iOS-Android-Arc-Search) links iOS and Android installations and describes Arc Sync. It also marks some pinning functions as iOS-only, so identical behavior across both phones should not be assumed.

Do not confuse it with the deprecated Arc Mobile Companion app. Nor should an alternative article about Arc Search automatically become a list of desktop browsers that imitate Arc's sidebar. This comparison focuses on what happens when you search, read, save, and open links on a phone.

There is a development-status qualification. [Arc's desktop homepage](https://arc.net/) says Arc receives Chromium updates only and directs readers to Dia for active security patches. That desktop notice does not prove that every mobile feature has been shut down. The [Android release notes](https://resources.arc.net/hc/en-us/articles/27306904379031-Arc-Search-for-Android-Release-Notes) include August 7, 2026 URL-spoofing fixes and Android compatibility updates. The [iOS store listing](https://apps.apple.com/in/app/arc-search-find-it-faster/id6472513080) also records recent bug and security improvements. Download availability and fixes are evidence of availability, not a guaranteed future feature roadmap.

## When changing one setting is enough

Keep Arc Search if its compact interface, reading flow, and tab cleanup are why you use it. If your only objection is the default AI search, investigate a setting before migrating. Arc's [search-engine guide](https://resources.arc.net/hc/en-us/articles/25482594475031-How-To-Set-a-Default-Search-Engine-in-Arc-Search) describes changing Browse for Me to another listed engine, including conventional search options.

Consider switching when you need a particular mobile extension, a different desktop browser ecosystem, stronger control over saved material, or an AI answer app with a different research workflow. These are specific requirements. We do not infer widespread failures from individual store reviews or claim that any listed tool is more accurate on every query.

## A shortlist by the job it replaces

The comparison uses official product, support, store, and pricing pages checked October 8, 2026. It is research-based. We did not measure search accuracy, browsing speed, battery use, or blocking performance. The examples are suggested evaluation tasks, not reported testing outcomes.

| Alternative | Main reason to try it | Cost and scope |
|---|---|---|
| Brave | Mobile browser with built-in blocking | Free core browser; optional services separate |
| DuckDuckGo Browser | Simple protection and browsing-data controls | Free browser; optional subscriptions separate |
| Firefox | Sync and compatible Android add-ons | Free browser; phone platforms differ |
| Safari | iPhone browsing within Apple's ecosystem | Included with supported Apple devices |
| Microsoft Edge | Browser with built-in Copilot assistance | Free browser; feature access can vary |
| Perplexity mobile app | Cited answers and follow-up research | Free Standard tier; optional Pro; partial replacement |

## Brave: replace the everyday mobile browser

[Brave](https://brave.com/) offers free browsing on iOS and Android as well as desktop platforms. Its built-in Shields block ads and trackers. This makes it a relevant candidate when the part of Arc Search you value is a cleaner view of ordinary websites.

The browser's optional products, such as its VPN, are separate. You do not need to purchase a VPN simply to use the core browser. Avoid comparing an optional paid-service price with Arc Search's free download as if those were equal packages.

Brave's [Shields documentation](https://support.brave.com/hc/en-us/articles/360022973471-What-is-Shields) describes protection controls and warns that stricter settings can break sites. That is a useful tradeoff to evaluate on your phone: a checkout, embedded video, or sign-in can matter more than a large blocked-request count.

Choose Brave if you want a full replacement for opening links and visiting sites, with blocking built in. Skip it as an assumed reproduction of Browse for Me's generated answer pages or Arc's desktop Spaces. The browser can solve your everyday browsing requirement without matching every Arc interaction. Start with default protections and test the websites you need rather than enabling the strictest option everywhere.

## DuckDuckGo Browser: simplify protection and clearing

[DuckDuckGo Browser](https://duckduckgo.com/app) is a free app for iOS and Android, with Mac and Windows versions also available. The current product page describes tracker protection, cookie pop-up handling, and a control for deleting browsing data. It also presents AI chat as optional, which is a different interaction from assuming every address-bar search must become an AI answer.

The advantage for an Arc Search user is a relatively direct protection workflow. It can be a good fit when you want to open a page, read it, and clear temporary browsing data without assembling several add-ons. The paid subscription protections are separate from the free browser.

The tradeoff is that clearing data is a deliberate action. Check what the app preserves and removes before clearing an active sign-in or research session. A simple control is only useful if you understand its effect on the material you wanted to retain.

Choose DuckDuckGo for ordinary mobile browsing with simpler privacy controls. Skip it as an assumed replacement for all Arc Sync structures or every Browse for Me result. Changing your search engine to DuckDuckGo inside Arc Search is also different from installing DuckDuckGo's browser. If changing search alone fixes the problem, a full browser migration may be unnecessary.

## Firefox: use sync and Android add-ons as the deciding features

[Firefox's mobile offering](https://www.firefox.com/en-US/mobile/) includes iOS and Android browsers with tracking protection, reader mode, and syncing of tabs, passwords, and bookmarks. The core browser is free. It is worth considering when desktop Firefox is already part of your routine or you want to move toward that ecosystem.

The more specific advantage on Android is extension support. Mozilla's [Android add-on guide](https://support.mozilla.org/en-US/kb/find-and-install-add-ons-firefox-android) describes installing compatible extensions and reviewing their requested permissions. It also notes that some add-ons are incompatible. Verify the one you need rather than assuming the whole desktop collection will work.

That Android guide is not evidence that Firefox on an iPhone accepts the same extensions. Platform differences are part of the decision, especially if your reason for leaving Arc Search is one particular add-on.

Choose Firefox for a conventional browsing and syncing workflow, with supported Android customization where relevant. Skip it as a guaranteed clone of Arc Search's AI answer format or desktop pinning arrangement. In a trial, confirm that a tab you save on the phone appears where you expect on your own desktop setup. Having a sync feature does not mean it maps Arc Spaces automatically.

## Safari: the first iPhone candidate for an Apple workflow

[Safari](https://www.apple.com/safari/) is included on supported Apple devices and offers reader-oriented browsing, Tab Groups, profiles, and App Store extensions. It is a sensible first comparison if you use an iPhone, iPad, and Mac and want those devices to remain central to how you browse.

There is no separate Safari browser subscription. Commercial extensions or other services can cost extra. Verify that your required extension exists for Safari on the device in question, rather than assuming a Mac version means the same functions on an iPhone.

Safari's strongest reason for inclusion is integration and organization inside the Apple ecosystem. It does not need to imitate Arc Search's search-first interface to be useful. Someone who wants saved research divided into named groups may prefer that organization, while someone who likes Arc's temporary-tab habit may find it unnecessary.

Choose Safari when you want a full iPhone browser and Apple-device continuity. Skip it as the answer for an Android phone or as a promised one-for-one Browse for Me replacement. Try your password autofill, reading, downloads, and saved-tab routine before deciding whether the built-in option already does enough. No battery ranking or performance claim is implied here.

## Microsoft Edge: investigate AI assistance in a browser

[Microsoft's mobile Edge page](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/edge/mobile) advertises built-in Copilot, summarization, and assistance across tabs, alongside normal mobile browsing. The page also notes that supported extensions differ by operating system. This makes Edge a more relevant candidate than a desktop-only AI browser when the question is what to install on your phone.

Core Edge browsing is free. Do not assume that every advertised AI function has identical availability, limits, or account requirements in every country and phone build. A feature's product-page presence is not a verified unlimited allowance. This comparison does not quote a paid Copilot plan as a requirement for ordinary Edge browsing.

Choose Edge if you want to investigate AI assistance while keeping pages and browser tasks in one app. For example, ask it to summarize a public article, then compare the summary with the original. That is a proposed trial, not evidence that it outperforms Browse for Me.

Skip Edge if the main goal is an interface with very few additional controls. Also inspect account and sync settings before using it for work. InPrivate clears certain local browsing data when tabs close; it should not be read as a guarantee that websites or online AI services receive no information.

## Perplexity mobile app: replace the answer workflow only

[Perplexity](https://www.perplexity.ai/hub) offers an answer engine on the web and in iOS and Android apps. Its [official iOS listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/perplexity-ai-search-chat/id1668000334) describes cited answers and follow-up threads. It is relevant when Browse for Me's synthesized answers, rather than Arc Search's browser controls, are your main reason to look elsewhere.

The Standard tier is free. The official product FAQ lists Pro at US$20 per month or US$200 per year. Those are separate billing choices, not the same monthly commitment. App-store and regional checkout amounts can differ. The [plan guide](https://www.perplexity.ai/help-center/en/articles/11187416-which-perplexity-subscription-plan-is-right-for-you) describes limited Pro searches and uploads on Standard, with higher access on paid tiers. We do not promise a fixed unlimited advanced-model allowance because current documentation qualifies access.

This is a partial replacement. The Perplexity answer app should not be treated as a full transfer of Arc Search's tabs, default-browser behavior, or Arc Spaces. Perplexity's Comet browser is another product and is outside this app comparison.

Choose the app to evaluate a research-and-follow-up workflow. Skip it as the sole solution when your real requirement is opening every phone link in a conventional browser. Citations help you inspect evidence, but you still need to open sources and check whether they support the answer.

## Compare an AI answer with the underlying pages

A useful trial is a question whose answer you can check: a product's official compatibility, a venue's published opening hours, or a documented software feature. Keep the query the same, record the date, and open the cited official pages. Look for missing qualifications, stale dates, and a source that says something different from the summary.

Also test an ordinary browsing task that has nothing to do with AI, such as signing in, downloading a PDF, or handing a link to another app. An answer tool can handle a question well while being a poor substitute for that routine.

Decide what information you are comfortable sending to an online service before pasting private material into a prompt. Browser tracking protection and an AI service's handling of submitted content are separate questions. Inspect the particular account's data settings and policy if that affects your choice.

There is no measured accuracy winner in this article. The recommendation is to choose the workflow that helps you find and verify useful information, rather than the answer that sounds most confident.

## Save the important links before changing defaults

Arc Search's temporary tabs and archives deserve an inventory before migration. Save the pages you want to keep as original URLs or in a supported destination. Do not assume a generated answer page is a permanent, portable bookmark.

If you also use desktop Arc, its [export guide](https://resources.arc.net/hc/en-us/articles/25583851606039-How-Do-I-Export-my-Pinned-and-Favorite-Tabs-in-Arc-to-Another-Browser) says there is no current direct export of pinned and favorite tabs. It describes placing them in a folder and using Copy All Links for manual saving. This is a desktop workaround, not a mobile export feature or an automatic recreation of Spaces in another browser.

Keep password migration separate from tab migration. Check your existing password manager's supported phone autofill and browser setup, and confirm important sign-ins before removing Arc Search. A saved URL does not transfer the session or recovery credentials.

Set the new browser as default after a small trial. Then test a link from mail, a messaging app, and an app that opens its own internal browser. The operating system's default setting does not guarantee every embedded web view behaves the same way. Keep the old app until the material you need is safely accessible.

## Questions before choosing

### Which alternative is closest to Browse for Me?

Perplexity's answer app is relevant for cited search and follow-ups, while Edge offers AI assistance within a browser. Neither is established here as an exact copy or an accuracy winner. Choose by whether you need answers or a full browsing app.

### Can I keep Arc Search and stop using AI as the default search?

Arc's official guide describes changing the default search engine on iOS and Android. Try that first if the default answer workflow is your only concern.

### Is Arc Search no longer available?

Official mobile pages still provide download links, and mobile records include fixes. Arc's desktop site has a separate reduced-update notice. Availability should not be turned into a promise of future feature development, but calling the mobile app entirely unavailable is unsupported by these sources.

### Which choice works for both iPhone and Android?

Brave, DuckDuckGo, Firefox, Edge, and Perplexity offer relevant phone apps. Features can differ between platforms. Safari is the Apple-device choice, and Perplexity's answer app is only a partial browser substitute.

### Will Arc Spaces move to another mobile browser?

Do not assume that. Save essential original links and review the destination's supported import and organization controls. Arc's desktop copy-links workaround does not recreate Spaces or automatically migrate phone archives.

## Replace the part that matters

Start with Brave or DuckDuckGo for built-in mobile protection, Firefox for its browsing ecosystem, or Safari for an iPhone-centered routine. Investigate Edge when AI assistance inside the browser is the goal, and Perplexity when the answer workflow matters most. Keep Arc Search if a setting change solves the problem. If you are also reconsidering desktop browsing, our [Orion Browser alternatives guide](/alternatives/orion-browser-alternatives/) covers that broader device-and-extension decision.
