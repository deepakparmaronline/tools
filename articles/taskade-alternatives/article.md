# 6 Taskade alternatives for projects, apps and AI workflows

Notion and ClickUp are useful Taskade alternatives when your priority is shared knowledge or team projects. Asana suits formal handoffs, Airtable suits records and operational apps, n8n suits agent automations, and Retool suits internal interfaces over existing data. Choose by the part of Taskade you actually use: a project manager may replace your task boards without replacing a Genesis app or its agents. For a small team already running those components together, staying with Taskade can make sense. Compare required seats, AI usage and rebuilding work before treating any free tier as an equivalent replacement.

## Taskade alternatives at a glance

The following shortlist separates project and knowledge work from app and automation work. These are workflow recommendations, not a ranking of identical products.

| Alternative | Best replacement need | Entry and billing context | Main switching tradeoff |
|---|---|---|---|
| Notion | Docs, wikis and page-based project databases | Free; paid per member; agent credits can add cost | You design the workspace structure and rebuild automations |
| ClickUp | Assigned tasks, tracking and project operations | Free; Unlimited $7/user/month annually | AI costs and configuration add to the base plan |
| Asana | Requests, handoffs and coordinated delivery | Free for two; Starter $10.99/user/month annually | Paid seat minimum; portfolio features need Advanced |
| Airtable | Linked records and operational interfaces | Free; Team $20/billable user/month annually | Data model, editor seats and usage limits need planning |
| n8n | Integrations and AI agent workflows | Hosted Starter €20/month annually; Community self-hosting | Requires workflow ownership and a separate team interface |
| Retool | Internal apps over databases and APIs | Free up to five; Team bills builders and internal users | Low plans exclude external users; apps need rebuilding |

Figures are public web prices checked October 8, 2026, before tax and optional usage charges. Annual prices are monthly equivalents of an annual commitment. Details and official links appear below. A free project plan, a free prototype allowance and a self-hosted software edition solve different budgeting problems.

## When keeping Taskade makes sense

[Taskade](https://www.taskade.com/) combines collaborative projects with AI agents, automations and Genesis app building. If your intake form, project records, assistant and customer-facing app already share one workspace, replacing only the task list can leave the rest of that process behind.

The current [Free plan](https://www.taskade.com/pricing) includes two users and up to three apps. Its 11,000 AI credits are a one-time grant earned through account verification and the first app build, not a renewed monthly allowance. Taskade also says ordinary app visits, projects, synchronization and non-AI automations do not consume AI credits. Reaching an AI credit limit does not mean existing apps stop serving visitors.

Keep it if that combined setup meets your needs and you can predict its AI usage. A switch to separate docs, automation and app services creates several subscriptions and several places to manage access. That may still be worthwhile, but the reason should be a concrete capability or operating requirement.

Reasons to evaluate a replacement include:

- Your team mainly needs project reporting, assigned work and predictable handoffs.
- Documents and shared knowledge matter more than generated apps.
- Customer or inventory data needs a deliberately designed record model.
- A technical operator wants to own integration logic and deployment.
- Your app should work directly with an existing database or internal API.

These are decision criteria, not allegations of defects. Begin with one workflow that Taskade cannot handle in the way you need.

## How these six options were selected

This comparison uses official product, pricing and documentation pages. It is research, not hands-on testing of app generation, imports or AI agent reliability. Candidates were selected for different parts of Taskade's current scope, then assessed for billing, ownership effort and the work that a switch would leave unfinished.

The useful question is specific: “Can this handle our request intake, data updates and reviewer approval?” “Does it have AI?” is too broad. A drafting assistant, a scheduled agent and an app-building assistant can all use that label while doing different jobs.

## 1. Notion for documents and shared knowledge

[Notion](https://www.notion.com/product) is the first option to consider when your Taskade workspace is mostly briefs, meeting notes, reference material and projects linked to those documents. Pages and databases let a team keep the explanation of its work beside the records it tracks.

For example, an editorial team could create a publication database with an owner, status, deadline and linked brief. The advantage is a consistent knowledge structure that people can browse and maintain. That is a different starting point from rebuilding an agent-driven CRM or a standalone customer portal.

The [Free plan](https://www.notion.com/pricing) provides unlimited pages and blocks for an individual, while collaboration with two or more members has limits. Paid plans bill per member. The pricing page's monthly/yearly selector was not unambiguous in the extracted view, so check the selected billing cycle before using its displayed base price in a budget.

AI also needs its own plan check. Free and Plus offer a trial of Notion AI; Business includes Notion Agent and other AI workspace features. Custom Agents have a separate credit model, advertised at $10 per 1,000 monthly Notion credits after the trial. Do not assume an included interactive agent makes every autonomous workflow unlimited.

**Tradeoff:** someone must choose database properties, views, permissions and conventions. Existing Taskade prompts and triggers should be treated as specifications to rebuild, rather than a system that transfers with a document.

**Skip Notion if** your main requirement is reproducing a deployed Genesis app, or your team wants a ready-defined task process without owning workspace design.

## 2. ClickUp for task operations and time tracking

[ClickUp](https://clickup.com/) is a sensible shortlist choice when Taskade is chiefly the place where people receive work, track deadlines and report progress. Its combination of tasks, docs and dashboards supports teams that want operational information attached to assigned work.

A client services team, for example, could define request statuses, record effort and review overdue deliverables in one project process. The reason to switch is that task model and reporting need, rather than the mere presence of an AI assistant. Decide which information a manager actually needs before adding fields and views.

ClickUp has a Free Forever plan. [Unlimited](https://clickup.com/pricing) costs $7 per user per month billed yearly, equivalent to $84 per user for the year, or $10 per user on monthly billing. Native time tracking and custom fields are listed in that paid tier. Advanced reporting and other capabilities may require a higher plan.

The same pricing page sells AI separately from the base project subscription. Budget the AI package and any agent or automation usage needed for your workflow; the entry seat price alone is not the price of every AI feature.

**Tradeoff:** defining statuses, ownership rules and useful dashboards takes organizational effort. A feature-rich workspace helps only if the team maintains those conventions. Moving task records also does not reconstruct a Taskade app or its agent logic.

**Skip ClickUp if** you mainly need a small knowledge base, or a live app rather than a project delivery workspace. Avoid adopting additional configuration merely because the interface offers it.

## 3. Asana for requests and cross-team handoffs

[Asana](https://asana.com/) fits teams whose core problem is getting work from request to owner to completion. It is worth evaluating when a Taskade project has become a collection of handoffs that different departments need to understand consistently.

A practical trial would follow a campaign request through intake, scheduling, dependencies and delivery. Choose the required plan from that process. Do not buy a lower tier expecting every portfolio or resource-management feature to be present.

The current [Personal plan](https://asana.com/pricing) supports two users with unlimited tasks and projects. Starter costs $10.99 per user per month billed annually, or $13.49 on monthly billing. Starter lists forms, timeline/Gantt views, reporting and automation. Portfolios and broader resource management are listed on Advanced.

Asana's official [subscription guidance](https://help.asana.com/s/article/asana-subscriptions-and-pricing?language=en_US) specifies a two-seat minimum for paid subscriptions. At the annual Starter rate, that is at least $263.76 for two seats per year before tax, rather than a single $10.99 monthly-equivalent seat.

The current pricing page also includes AI Teammates, Asana Dash and AI Studio in paid plans. Check the account's allocations and usage terms for the particular AI product you intend to run; “included” should not become an assumption that every agent workload has no additional cost.

**Tradeoff:** a coordinated project system covers the work surrounding an app, but does not automatically replace the app. Rebuilding a customer-facing tool is a separate requirement.

**Skip Asana if** your use is mostly personal notes, or your reason for leaving Taskade is to control app data and integration execution yourself.

## 4. Airtable for structured records and operational apps

[Airtable](https://www.airtable.com/) belongs on the shortlist when Taskade holds information that needs explicit relationships: clients linked to jobs, assets linked to campaigns, or requests linked to reviewers. Its record-based approach, interfaces and AI-assisted building support workflows organized around a shared data model.

For an equipment checkout process, start with equipment, borrowers and loans as separate records. Then design the views and interface people need to request an item and record a return. The advantage is making the structure of the information deliberate, instead of relying on increasingly complicated task names.

Airtable's [Free plan](https://airtable.com/pricing) is intended for lightweight needs. Team is $20 per user per month billed annually, equivalent to $240 per billable seat per year. On Team and Business, users with edit permission in at least one workspace base are charged; read-only collaborators, form submissions and share links are not.

Record, attachment, automation and API limits also matter. [AI billing documentation](https://support.airtable.com/articles/3378106230-airtable-ai-billing) describes included but limited credits pooled across the relevant workspace or organization. Free users must upgrade to purchase more AI credits. The AI allowance is separate from the reason to choose a relational record system.

**Tradeoff:** you need someone to own the data model and permissions. Importing rows is only an early step; existing agent instructions, app behavior and automation rules need independent review.

**Skip Airtable if** most of your work is long-form writing or a simple checklist. It is also the wrong shortcut if you expect an existing Genesis app to move over without reconstruction.

## 5. n8n for integrations and AI agent workflows

[n8n](https://n8n.io/) is the more relevant alternative when the part of Taskade you value is the sequence of actions: receive data, call a service, classify it, update a record and notify a person. It combines visual workflows with code and AI agent steps.

Consider a lead-routing process that takes a form submission, checks a CRM, proposes a category and asks a human to approve the next action. Evaluate each integration and failure path. The benefit of this choice is owning an explicit workflow, rather than adding another task board around the same automation problem.

The hosted [Starter plan](https://n8n.io/pricing) is €20 per month billed annually, or €240 for the year, with 2,500 monthly workflow executions and unlimited steps per execution. Those execution units are not comparable to Taskade AI credits. An available model connection or AI assistant allowance also does not establish that every provider call is free.

The self-hostable Community edition is another route, with its own licensing terms. It shifts responsibility for hosting, updates, backups and availability to your operator. Price provider or gateway usage and connected services alongside that work.

**Tradeoff:** n8n is an automation component. It does not by itself replace the shared notes, project management and end-user interface you may be leaving. A team may need a separate place to review records and approvals.

**Skip n8n if** nobody can maintain integrations or troubleshoot failed runs. Choose it for control of workflow execution, not as an assumed zero-cost version of the complete Taskade workspace.

## 6. Retool for internal apps over existing data

[Retool](https://retool.com/) is useful when your Taskade alternative needs to be an internal tool that works with databases and APIs you already have. The focus is the interface employees use to inspect and update operational data.

For example, a support team could evaluate a refund-review screen that retrieves an order, displays relevant information and calls an approved API action. That is a stronger reason to try an internal app platform than simply wanting a new AI builder. Confirm the data source, access rules and required actions first.

The [Free plan](https://retool.com/pricing) supports up to five users and includes 250 pooled AI credits each month. It is a limited starting point, not unrestricted AI use. Free and Team exclude external users and embedded apps, which matters if your current Taskade app is a customer portal.

Team lists $10 per builder per month and $5 per internal user per month, billed annually. A builder is someone who creates or edits an app or workflow during the billing cycle. Calculate the roles your actual team needs, rather than multiplying one headline rate by everyone.

**Tradeoff:** connecting data and designing the interface requires a builder who understands the workflow. A Taskade project export is not a Retool application, and you should not assume its prompts, permissions or automations will carry across.

**Skip Retool if** your main need is collaborative notes or personal task planning. If external customers need access, evaluate the eligible higher-tier terms before building around the free allowance.

## Choose by the work you need to preserve

List the components used by one real process: documents, project records, agents, triggers, connected accounts and the screen people interact with. Mark which are essential.

If only documents and task records matter, start with Notion or ClickUp. If handoffs dominate, try Asana. If the records and their relationships are the product, try Airtable. If the sequence of integrations matters most, examine n8n. If staff need an interface over existing systems, examine Retool.

Then build a budget from billable roles and actual usage. For an illustrative team of five, ask whether all five need editing rights, whether anyone is only a reader, and who will build workflows. Separately estimate AI work and automation volume. A thousand credits, a thousand requests and a thousand executions are different units; convert them into your expected process rather than comparing their raw counts.

Include rebuilding and maintenance time. A cheaper subscription can be a more expensive operating choice if your team must replace a working app and maintain several integrations.

## Moving from Taskade without losing the process

Taskade's [export guide](https://www.taskade.com/learn/import/exporting) documents Markdown and CSV project exports on every plan, with additional image, text and PDF formats on paid plans. The [Markdown instructions](https://help.taskade.com/en/articles/8958614-export-as-markdown) describe exporting an individual project from its menu. These are useful starting points, not evidence of a complete portable app and agent runtime.

Use a small migration before moving the whole workspace:

1. Export one representative project and keep an untouched copy.
2. Inventory attachments, comments, assignees, dates, recurring work and linked records separately.
3. Record agent instructions, knowledge sources, triggers, integrations and app screens as a rebuild specification.
4. Map exported fields to the destination and inspect the result; do not assume every relationship survives.
5. Recreate one automation with a test account, including a failed request and a human review step.
6. Confirm who can read, edit and trigger actions before connecting real services.
7. Move new work after the pilot succeeds, keeping the old material available until the team accepts the replacement.

For n8n, [workflow JSON export/import](https://docs.n8n.io/build/manage-workflows/export-and-import.md) helps manage n8n workflows after reconstruction. Its documentation warns that exports can include credential names and IDs, and that HTTP nodes can contain authentication headers. Review files before sharing them. This is not a universal Taskade importer.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the best free Taskade alternative for one person?

Notion Free is a useful starting point for documents and project databases. ClickUp Free Forever suits a task-oriented workspace, while current Asana Personal supports two collaborators. Select the workflow first, then check its free limits. None of those free tiers establishes a complete replacement for every Taskade app and agent.

### Is Notion AI included in the free plan?

Free and Plus provide a trial. Business includes Notion Agent and other AI features, while autonomous Custom Agents use a separate credit model. Choose the relevant feature and billing plan before assuming the whole AI workflow is included.

### Can ClickUp or Asana replace Taskade Genesis?

They can replace parts of the project process, such as assignment and delivery tracking. A deployed app needs its own interface, data access and automation review. Treat that as a separate migration requirement rather than assuming project imports reproduce app behavior.

### Is self-hosting n8n cheaper than using Taskade?

It depends on hosting, maintenance, provider usage and the other workspace components you still need. Community software availability removes one type of subscription requirement, but does not remove the operating work. Compare the cost of your complete process.

### Can Retool Free run a customer portal?

Its current pricing excludes external users and embedded apps on Free and Team. Those plans fit internal evaluation. A customer-facing requirement needs an eligible plan and a review of external-user pricing before you commit.

### Will a Taskade CSV export preserve my agents and apps?

The documented export is a project format. Do not assume a CSV includes executable agent instructions, connected credentials or a portable app interface. Inventory those components and validate them separately during a pilot.

## Start with one replacement workflow

Choose the option that addresses your actual switching reason, then rebuild one representative process on the necessary plan. Check task and data integrity, permissions, failed actions and the resulting bill. Keep Taskade if the proposed switch adds tools without improving the work you need to do.
