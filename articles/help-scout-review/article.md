# Help Scout Review: Features, Limits and Fit

Help Scout is a good fit for teams that want a shared customer inbox, a connected help center and clear ownership of customer conversations. It is less suited to teams that need complex ticket fields, strict queue controls or a large service desk built around many custom processes. The main decision is whether its email-first workflow matches the way your support team works.

## Help Scout review: the short verdict

Help Scout brings email, chat, social messages, knowledge base content and customer context into one support workspace. Its main strength is the connection between Inbox, Docs and Beacon. Agents can see previous customer conversations, add private notes, assign ownership and share help articles without moving between several systems.

The platform is worth considering for SaaS companies, online stores, service businesses and small support teams that value a readable conversation history. It is not automatically the right choice for every team. A business with a large call centre, complex case types, advanced approval paths or strict agent-only visibility should compare it with a more configurable ticketing platform.

## What Help Scout does

Help Scout is a customer support platform built around conversations. The Inbox is the working area for customer email and connected channels. Docs is the hosted knowledge base. Beacon is the website or in-app support widget. Workflows handle repeatable actions, while AI tools can help agents write and understand replies or answer customers from approved sources.

This design is useful when support is mostly a conversation between a customer and a person on the team. It keeps customer context beside the message rather than treating every contact as a disconnected ticket. The [shared Inbox guide](https://docs.helpscout.com/article/1581-what-is-shared-inbox) explains collision detection, private notes and customer history.

## Features that matter in daily support

### 1. Shared Inbox and customer context

The Inbox gives teammates a shared place to read and answer customer messages. Agents can assign a conversation to a user, leave a private note and check what another teammate is working on. Collision detection helps prevent two people from writing a reply to the same message at the same time.

The customer sidebar also shows earlier interactions. That context can reduce repeated questions when a customer contacts the business again. It does not remove the need for clear ownership rules. Teams still need to decide who handles new messages, when a conversation should be reassigned and who follows up after an internal note.

### 2. Docs knowledge base

Docs lets a team publish help articles in a searchable site. Articles can be grouped into collections and categories. The same content can support self-service on the public help center and inside Beacon.

A knowledge base is only useful when it stays current. Someone must own article reviews, product-change updates and the removal of old instructions. If your company has several products or brands, check the number of Docs sites included in the plan and the cost of extra sites before designing the structure.

### 3. Beacon for website support

Beacon is an embeddable widget that can connect a website visitor with help content, a contact form or live chat. It can also offer Scout, Help Scout's customer-facing AI agent, when the relevant setup and plan allow it. A Beacon is linked to one Inbox. If live chat is enabled, the email form remains available as a fallback when the team is not online. The [Beacon setup guide](https://docs.helpscout.com/article/1232-create-a-new-beacon) lists the setup stages.

The pricing table shows different Beacon allowances by plan. Check the current plan comparison before creating widgets for multiple brands or products. A single widget on a small site is a different need from several branded support experiences.

### 4. Workflows and saved replies

Workflows use conditions and actions to organize, assign or reply to conversations. Automatic workflows run when a conversation matches their conditions. Manual workflows run when a teammate chooses to apply them. Help Scout lists workflows on Standard, Plus and Pro. The [workflow guide](https://docs.helpscout.com/article/22-get-started-with-workflows) explains the setup process and permissions.

Saved replies help agents answer common questions with approved wording. They are useful for order updates, account access instructions and repeated policy questions. A team should review them when policies change. A saved reply that contains an old price or an expired process can create more work than it saves.

### 5. Reporting and integrations

Help Scout provides reports for volume, responses and customer happiness. The plan comparison lists different reporting history by plan. Teams should check whether the history and report filters match their own reporting needs before choosing a tier.

The pricing page lists access to more than 100 apps, but the number of integrations available on a plan varies. It also lists API access, webhooks and OAuth apps. If a support process depends on a CRM, billing system or issue tracker, confirm the exact integration and plan access rather than relying on the headline integration count.

### 6. AI tools and MCP access

Help Scout separates AI assistance for agents from its customer-facing AI agent. Agent tools can help with drafts, summaries and editing. Envoy is charged by resolution after the trial period. The current pricing page lists US$0.75 per resolution and says a resolution is counted when Scout resolves a conversation without human help. A customer who asks for more help or escalates does not count as a billable resolution. Checked November 22, 2026. [Envoy pricing and billing rules](https://www.helpscout.com/pricing/).

Help Scout also documents an MCP connection for AI assistants. New connections are read-only and follow the permissions of the Help Scout user who connects. API access is available on Standard, Plus and Pro, while reporting tools require Plus or Pro. A Docs API key is needed for knowledge base tools. Read the [official MCP setup and limits](https://docs.helpscout.com/article/1779-help-scout-mcp-server) before connecting customer data to an AI provider.

## Pricing and value

Help Scout's paid plans are charged per user. The annual-billed monthly rates are US$25 for Standard, US$45 for Plus and US$75 for Pro; month-to-month rates are US$30, US$54 and US$90 respectively. The page shows a 16% annual discount. The Free plan supports up to five users and 100 contacts per monthly cycle. These prices and limits were checked November 22, 2026 on the [official pricing page](https://www.helpscout.com/pricing/) and [Free plan guide](https://docs.helpscout.com/article/1597-free-plan).

Extra Inbox and Docs site charges can affect the total. Envoy adds a separate usage cost when it resolves a conversation. Pro also has a ten-user minimum. A small team should not select Pro only because it includes more controls. Compare the features you need with the actual seat count and the likely add-on use.

Use the full [Help Scout pricing guide](/help-scout/pricing/) for the plan table, included counts and cost examples. It explains the difference between included Inbox quantities and the maximum number of Inboxes a plan permits.

## Pros and trade-offs

**Where Help Scout can work well**

- Shared customer conversations, internal notes and assignment are part of the same Inbox.
- Docs and Beacon connect help content with the support conversation.
- The Free plan gives a small team a way to begin without a paid seat subscription.
- Official import support covers several common help desk providers, including Freshdesk, Intercom, Zendesk and Zoho Desk.
- API and MCP access give technical teams ways to connect support data to other systems.

**What to check before buying**

- Free stops replies after 100 contacts in a monthly cycle. This is a hard operating limit, not only a reporting threshold.
- The standard import does not transfer users, Inbox settings, help center articles, saved replies, macros, automation rules or some custom fields and statuses.
- Report exports do not include conversation thread contents. Other data needs the Inbox API or Docs API.
- Custom fields are available on Plus and Pro, and each field belongs to a specific Inbox. Moving a conversation to another Inbox does not carry its custom field data.
- New MCP connections are read-only. They cannot reply to customers or edit Help Scout records.

These are not reasons to reject the product in every case. They are planning questions. A team with simple email support may not need advanced ticket fields. A team with strict data migration or reporting requirements may need a separate export project before it can switch.

## How to judge the fit before you move

Map one normal customer request from the first email to the final reply. Include the steps that happen outside the support inbox, such as checking an order, asking an engineer for help or updating a customer record. Then check which steps Help Scout handles directly and which need an integration or a separate system.

Run the same exercise for a complicated request. Include an escalation, a custom data field, a second team and a follow-up after several days. This will show whether a shared conversation model is enough or whether your team depends on more detailed ticket controls. The official feature list can show what a plan includes, but it cannot decide how your team should route work.

Before switching, also prepare a migration inventory. Count the conversations, customers, tags, attachments, knowledge articles, saved replies and rules you need to keep. Ask the vendor how each item will move. The [official import guide](https://docs.helpscout.com/article/1495-import-emails-or-tickets-into-help-scout) explains what the standard tool does and does not transfer.

## Who should choose Help Scout?

Choose Help Scout when the team needs a shared Inbox, customer history, Docs and a support widget, but does not need a large service-management system. It can be a sensible fit for a small SaaS support team, a service business or an online store that handles most requests through email and chat.

Keep your current platform or compare a broader help desk if your team relies on complex ticket types, many custom statuses, detailed routing rules, large knowledge base migrations or full conversation-thread exports. Do not judge the fit by the number of features alone. Map a normal customer request from first message to resolution and check whether every step is supported on the plan you would buy.

## Help Scout review questions

**Is Help Scout good for a small business?**

It can suit a small business that wants a shared Inbox and help center. Check the Free plan's 100-contact monthly limit and the paid seat price against your real support volume.

**Does Help Scout have a free plan?**

Yes. The official Free plan includes up to five users, one Inbox, one Docs site and 100 contacts per monthly cycle. Checked November 22, 2026. [Free plan details](https://docs.helpscout.com/article/1597-free-plan).

**Does Help Scout include AI?**

It offers agent-side AI features and Scout through Beacon. Envoy is billed by successful AI resolution after the trial. New MCP connections are read-only.

**Can Help Scout replace Zendesk?**

It can replace some email-first support workflows, but the two products have different levels of ticketing and service operations. Compare routing, custom fields, channels, migration needs and total seat cost before moving.

**Can I export all Help Scout data?**

Not through one report export. Reports can be exported to CSV or XLSX, but conversation thread contents are excluded. The Inbox API and Docs API provide other data export paths. See the [official export options](https://docs.helpscout.com/article/466-data-export-options).
