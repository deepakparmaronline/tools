# 6 Shadow Alternatives for Meeting Notes and Capture

For a Shadow alternative, start with Granola if you want an AI notepad, Jamie for bot-free notes with explicit meeting allowances, Bluedot for a shared recording library, or Krisp when audio cleanup matters as well as notes. Fathom offers free individual recording and summaries, while Zoom's current AI offering is worth checking if your workplace already uses Zoom. These replace different parts of Shadow's workflow. This article covers the AI meeting assistant at shadow.do, not Shadow's cloud PC service. Choose by capture method, required context, data handling, and what you need to do after the call.

## Compare capture, not just summaries

| Alternative | Useful reason to shortlist | Pricing or free boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Granola | Combine your own notes with meeting context | Basic free with limited history; Business lists $14/user/month |
| Jamie | Bot-free online and in-person notes | Free: 10 notes/month, 30-minute meeting limit |
| Bluedot | Search and share a recording library | Free allowance is five meetings over the account lifetime |
| Krisp | Audio cleanup alongside meeting notes | Seven-day trial; Core $16/user/month or $8 annual equivalent |
| Fathom | Individual recording and summaries | Free individual plan; team plans have a two-user minimum |
| Zoom AI / ZoomMate | Evaluate an existing workplace platform first | Basic has small AI allowances; advanced license and credits separate |

We reviewed official product, pricing, and support pages on October 8, 2026. This comparison is based on research, not controlled transcription tests. We do not rank recognition accuracy, summary quality, or time saved. Six candidates cover note-centric, recording-centric, audio-focused, and platform-native needs.

## Reasons to keep Shadow

[Shadow](https://www.shadow.do/) captures meetings without a participant bot and offers customizable Skills, screen context, Markdown output, and webhooks. The vendor says transcription and meeting storage are local, while triggered AI Skills use LLM APIs. That distinction matters: local capture does not mean every optional AI action stays offline.

Its [free plan](https://www.shadow.do/pricing) lists unlimited transcription, audio recording, and smart screenshots, plus a two-week Plus trial without a card. The page displayed Plus at $8/month, but its extracted monthly/annual selector did not establish the billing commitment clearly enough for a like-for-like price comparison. Verify the selected term in the app.

Keep Shadow if these capture and output paths fit your Mac workflow. Consider switching for a mixed-device team, a shared cloud library, audio cleanup, or administrative controls. None of those requirements proves Shadow's notes are worse. They simply describe a different purchasing problem.

## 1. Granola for notes you shape yourself

[Granola](https://www.granola.ai/) uses computer audio without inviting a bot and combines meeting context with notes you write. The current website lists macOS, Windows, iOS, and Android. It suits someone who wants to mark important points during a call and then work from an enhanced notepad.

For example, you might jot down a decision and a disputed deadline rather than expect the software to infer which part of the meeting matters most. This is a different emphasis from selecting a Shadow Skill to generate a particular output.

**Pricing:** [Basic](https://www.granola.ai/pricing) is free with limited meeting history. Business lists $14 per user per month, adding unlimited history and notes plus advanced integrations. Confirm the billing term at checkout. Training opt-out is per user on Basic and Business; Enterprise adds a workspace-wide control.

**Tradeoff:** Limited history can undermine long-term recall even if note creation is sufficient. Skip the free tier as a permanent archive unless its actual history window meets your needs. If Shadow's captured screen context is essential, verify that capability separately instead of assuming every AI notepad interprets visuals.

## 2. Jamie for defined bot-free note allowances

[Jamie](https://www.meetjamie.ai/) captures online and in-person meetings without a virtual participant. It describes notes, meeting chat, and follow-up integrations. The vendor says audio is deleted after transcription and describes processing jurisdictions; those claims should be checked against the contract your organization receives.

It belongs on a shortlist where a team wants meeting knowledge and follow-up workflows rather than a personal screen-context assistant. An illustrative evaluation is turning a project review into notes that can be reviewed and sent to the team's document system.

**Pricing:** The [free plan](https://www.meetjamie.ai/pricing) allows ten AI notes per month with a 30-minute meeting-duration limit. Plus displays €21/month billed annually, with twenty notes per month and a two-hour limit. Pro displays €39/month billed annually, unlimited notes, and a five-hour limit. These annual equivalents are not month-to-month rates.

**Tradeoff:** Both note counts and meeting length matter. Skip Free for regular hour-long calls even if you have fewer than ten meetings. Check which integrations require Pro and whether the required team controls belong to a separate business offering.

## 3. Bluedot for recording and sharing workflows

[Bluedot](https://www.bluedothq.com/) offers bot-free capture, transcripts, summaries, and a searchable meeting library. It describes browser, Mac, Windows, and mobile capture routes, plus sharing, comments, clips, and integrations. This is relevant when colleagues need to revisit a meeting record rather than receive only your final summary.

A useful trial task is recording a demonstration, finding the relevant segment, and sharing it with the intended viewers. Inspect access settings as well as playback quality.

**Pricing:** [Free](https://www.bluedothq.com/pricing) provides **five meetings over the account lifetime**, not five per month. Basic displays $14/user/month for audio-only meetings with a one-hour recording limit. Pro displays $20/user/month with video and longer recording capability. The page has annual and monthly selectors; confirm which commitment produces those displayed prices before buying.

**Tradeoff:** Video storage and model interpretation of visual context are different capabilities. We did not establish that a recorded video makes every summary visually aware. Skip Basic if reviewing screen recordings is essential, and do not mistake the small free allowance for a durable daily-use plan.

## 4. Krisp for better audio plus meeting notes

[Krisp](https://krisp.ai/) combines noise cancellation with transcription, recordings, notes, and action items. Its meeting assistant is distinct from its call-center and developer products. It is a practical candidate when your problem includes noisy calls, not just remembering them afterward.

The site describes on-device English transcription and server-based transcription for additional languages. Check the mode used by your actual language and feature combination. A feature working locally does not establish that the complete workflow uses no cloud processing.

**Pricing:** The [current pricing page](https://krisp.ai/pricing/) offers a **seven-day trial**, rather than an unrestricted permanent free production plan. Core lists $16 per user per month, or $8 per user per month with annual billing. It includes meeting notes and noise cancellation, with a listed 10GB storage allowance. Higher plans add controls and integrations.

**Tradeoff:** A virtual audio device or capture configuration is another component to manage. Skip a switch motivated only by noise cancellation if your existing note workflow is otherwise suitable; evaluate whether the audio function can address the problem without replacing everything.

## 5. Fathom for a free individual recording workflow

[Fathom](https://www.fathom.ai/) focuses on recording, transcription, summaries, and sharing meeting information. Its [current pricing page](https://www.fathom.ai/pricing) lists unlimited recordings and transcriptions with instant summaries on the free individual plan. It also lists a choice of bot capture or bot-free capture, with the latter labeled beta.

This makes it a relevant candidate for someone who wants to revisit recordings and produce notes without starting with a paid individual plan. Verify that the capture route you want is available on your system; beta availability is not a guarantee of identical behavior across platforms.

**Pricing:** The free individual plan is separate from paid team capabilities. Team lists $15/user/month on annual billing or $19 monthly, with a **two-user minimum**. Business adds functions such as CRM field sync and coaching tools.

**Tradeoff:** A shared recording workflow is not the same as Shadow's local meeting record and customizable context Skills. Skip a team purchase based on the individual free feature list. Review retention, permissions, and the exact capture mode before standardizing across staff.

## 6. Zoom's AI offering for existing Zoom workplaces

[Zoom's current AI product page](https://www.zoom.com/en/products/ai-assistant/) presents ZoomMate, while some familiar workflows and older references use the AI Companion name. Review the current account entitlement rather than relying on an older claim that all AI features are included with every paid Zoom plan.

The official [My Notes page](https://www.zoom.com/en/products/ai-assistant/features/ai-note-taking/) describes note capture for Zoom, third-party platforms, and in-person conversations. That makes the existing workplace platform worth evaluating before adding a separate service.

**Pricing:** ZoomMate Basic lists three hosted-meeting summaries and three My Notes uses per month, alongside other small allowances. Advanced capabilities require an appropriate license, with credits used for some activity. A reliable numeric paid price was not visible in the researched page.

**Tradeoff:** Administrators and account entitlements affect availability. Skip it as a presumed universal free replacement across unlimited meetings. Ask your administrator which note features are enabled, what third-party capture requires, and whether retention and export rules meet your needs.

## Decide what must survive the switch

Start by separating capture from output. Capture may include system audio, microphone audio, video, screenshots, or a participant bot. Output may include a transcript, a short summary, a structured sales note, an editable document, or a CRM update. A replacement can match one stage and miss another.

If your calls involve product demonstrations or design reviews, include a visual-context requirement in the evaluation. Ask whether the system preserves a picture, records video for playback, or actually supplies visual information to the model producing the answer. These are three different mechanisms. Do not infer the third from the presence of the first two.

If your work is mostly spoken decisions, prioritize reviewable transcripts, speaker labels, names, dates, and action assignments. A fluent summary can still omit an objection or turn a proposal into a decision. Before sending it onward, check the portions that create obligations for other people.

For a team rollout, define who owns a meeting record, who can view it, and whether a colleague leaving the organization affects access. A personal notepad and an organization-managed library need different administrative arrangements.

## Compare the real monthly workload

Count typical meetings, their length, and the number of people who require paid seats. Then map those figures to the published limits. Ten notes with a short duration limit can be less useful than fewer recordings that cover your complete calls. A five-meeting lifetime allowance is suitable for evaluation, not recurring monthly work.

Include storage and integrations in the calculation. A low entry price does not establish that the plan can keep your recordings, connect to the required CRM, or expose administrative controls. Annual-billed prices represent a larger commitment than their monthly-looking display suggests.

Run the evaluation with representative, appropriately approved meetings. Test your microphone setup and conferencing app, then check whether capture starts and ends as expected. We did not conduct that test for this article, so no vendor receives a reliability score based on its marketing description.

## Export and review before migrating

Shadow describes Markdown and webhook output. Save the records you need while your current workflow remains available, and check the files you produce. A summary export may omit screenshots, timestamps, attachments, or the complete transcript. Decide which of those items you actually need to retain.

Recreate one important output template in the candidate app. Compare the fields, editability, and delivery destination. A generic integration logo does not prove the connector writes the correct CRM field or preserves your note structure. Review an example result before allowing automated updates.

Bot-free recording still captures other people's contributions. Follow the applicable meeting rules and your organization's notice and consent requirements. Configure sharing deliberately, review retention and training settings, and keep confidential meetings outside the trial until the proposed handling is accepted by the responsible people.

Keep the original tool available during evaluation, using one intended capture route at a time to avoid duplicate records. Switch only after required outputs, access, and export paths have been checked.

## Frequently asked questions

### Which Shadow alternative does not invite a meeting bot?

Granola, Jamie, and Bluedot describe bot-free capture. Krisp also offers a bot-free meeting assistant. Fathom's current page lists bot-free capture in beta as well as bot capture. Check the exact installed mode rather than assuming the product name establishes it.

### Does bot-free mean all processing stays on my computer?

No. Capture method and processing location are separate decisions. Shadow distinguishes local transcription from AI Skills that call APIs, and Krisp describes different transcription paths by language. Review the feature-specific data flow.

### Which option has a useful ongoing free plan?

Fathom's individual plan lists unlimited recording and transcription, and Granola Basic provides notes with limited history. Jamie Free has monthly note and duration limits. Bluedot's five-meeting lifetime allowance is primarily an evaluation boundary.

### Can a meeting recording replace Shadow's screen context?

Only if it meets your actual requirement. Playback, saved screenshots, and model-visible visual information are different. Ask the candidate to demonstrate the output you need from a screen-sharing meeting.

### Should I pay for another assistant if my company uses Zoom?

First check the current Zoom account entitlement and enabled note features. The researched ZoomMate Basic allowances are small, and advanced capabilities can require licensing and credits. Compare that actual entitlement with a separate service.

## Choose a capture and output pair

Shortlist Granola or Jamie for note-centered work, Bluedot or Fathom for recording and sharing, and Krisp when call audio is part of the problem. Evaluate Zoom's enabled features if it is already your workplace platform.

Keep Shadow when local capture, screen context, and your Skills meet the requirement. The next step is a limited evaluation of a representative call and a real output destination. Review the exported record and sharing controls before committing to a replacement.
