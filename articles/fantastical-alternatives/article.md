# 6 Fantastical Alternatives for Different Calendar Workflows

Apple Calendar is a useful Fantastical alternative when you want a familiar calendar without another app subscription. Google Calendar suits a Google-centered schedule, while Outlook fits Microsoft work accounts. BusyCal offers a configurable Mac experience with a direct perpetual license; Calendars by Readdle keeps events and tasks together on Apple devices. Morgen is worth considering when scheduling tasks across platforms matters more than reducing cost. Choose by the accounts you must edit, the devices you actually use, and the Fantastical features you would miss. A cheaper app can be an expensive switch if it breaks a shared calendar.

## Fantastical alternatives at a glance

| Alternative | Most useful for | Cost model | Main tradeoff |

| Apple Calendar | Everyday scheduling on Apple devices | Included app | Fewer specialist scheduling controls |
| Google Calendar | Google accounts and shared schedules | Free personal baseline; paid premium features | Different experience from a native multi-provider client |

Prices were checked on October 8, 2026. Published USD amounts exclude any applicable taxes and regional differences. The recommendations below are based on official documentation and workflow fit, rather than hands-on performance testing.

## What Fantastical already does well

Fantastical combines calendar accounts, natural-language event creation, task integrations and meeting scheduling. Its Openings and Proposals features matter if you regularly coordinate with people outside your organization. Moving to a simpler calendar can mean rebuilding that booking workflow elsewhere. [Fantastical product overview](https://flexibits.com/fantastical)

It also supports Windows alongside Apple platforms. A Windows laptop is therefore not, by itself, a reason to replace it. Check the exact feature you need on that platform rather than relying on older comparisons that describe an Apple-only product.

Flexibits offers a free edition and premium Individual, Family and Team plans, with a 14-day premium trial. The public pricing page did not expose verifiable numeric subscription amounts during research, so this guide does not substitute an old quote. [Flexibits plans](https://flexibits.com/pricing)

Keep Fantastical if its event entry, calendar sets, task connections and meeting links already work across your devices. Before changing products, try a simpler view or review which premium features you actually use. Paying for a tool that removes daily friction can make sense; paying for unused features is a different decision.

## How these six alternatives were selected

The shortlist covers six realistic ways to replace a calendar workflow. Three options start with the account ecosystems many readers already use. Two offer an alternative Apple calendar interface. One focuses on placing tasks into available time across desktop platforms.

Compatibility has two separate questions: can the app run on your device, and can it edit your calendar provider? A web calendar accessible on Windows may still be unsuitable for an iCloud-and-Exchange combination. A beautiful Mac client may still be unsuitable for an Android phone.

The suggested evaluation is to connect your actual accounts, inspect one shared calendar, change one recurring event and compare one invitation. These are checks for the reader to perform, not tests this article claims to have completed.

## 1. Apple Calendar: start with the included Apple option

Apple Calendar is the first alternative to evaluate when your calendar needs are straightforward and your everyday devices are Apple products. On Mac, it can bring iCloud, Google and other CalDAV account calendars into one place. That makes it more useful than a tool limited to your Apple account alone. [Apple account setup](https://support.apple.com/guide/calendar/add-calendars-and-calendar-accounts-icl4308d6701/mac)

For example, a person with a household calendar and a separate appointment calendar can assess whether the included app already provides enough visibility. If it does, an extra calendar-client bill may add little value.

**Cost:** the app is included with supported Apple operating systems. This does not mean a paid work account or every connected calendar service becomes free.

**Tradeoff:** it is an everyday calendar baseline, not a promise to reproduce Fantastical's booking links, calendar sets or every task integration. Assess event entry and recurring-event editing on the devices you use. An iCloud website on another operating system is a different experience from the native Apple app.

**Skip it if:** the proposed replacement needs to be the same native client on your Windows desktop and Android phone, or a specific Fantastical scheduling feature is central to your work.

Migration can be light if you are simply reconnecting existing accounts. There is no reason to export and reimport a Google calendar solely to change its display app.

## 2. Google Calendar: choose it for a Google-centered schedule

Google Calendar fits people whose appointments, invitations and shared schedules already live in Google. It reduces the number of services between the account holding your events and the interface you use to manage them. Google also documents calendar sharing, task scheduling and appointment booking. [Google Calendar overview](https://workspace.google.com/products/calendar/)

The strongest case is a small team or household that already shares Google calendars. Moving the interface to Google can be simpler than adding another client and troubleshooting which layer controls a setting.

**Cost:** a personal Google Account provides a free baseline. Premium appointment features depend on an eligible paid subscription. Google documents one booking page for personal accounts, with additional capabilities available through qualifying subscriptions.

**Tradeoff:** moving to Google's calendar environment is a bigger change for a person juggling several calendar providers. Seeing an external subscription is not equivalent to editing that account with full permissions. Verify the exact read-and-write arrangement before making it your only calendar.

**Skip it if:** your main need is a native Mac interface that preserves a mixed-provider workflow or your employer only permits an approved Microsoft client.

Use the trial period to inspect sharing permissions, default event visibility and which account receives new events. These settings matter more than matching Fantastical's colors.

## 3. Outlook: fit the calendar to Microsoft work

Outlook is a sensible replacement when email invitations and work availability already revolve around Microsoft. It brings mail and calendar into a shared working environment, so the decision is often about reducing app switching rather than acquiring a new scheduler. [Microsoft Outlook offer](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-365/outlook/outlook-for-windows)

Consider it for a worker who responds to most invitations from an organizational mailbox. Start with the edition approved by that organization and verify access to its calendars. A consumer account comparison does not establish what a managed Exchange account permits.

**Cost:** Microsoft offers free, ad-supported personal Outlook email and calendar apps. Microsoft 365 adds paid capabilities. Business account licensing and administration are separate from the free personal offer.

**Tradeoff:** Outlook is a family of experiences across web, desktop and mobile, and Microsoft's page explicitly qualifies platform feature differences. Do not assume the new Windows app, classic desktop Outlook and mobile Outlook behave identically.

**Skip it if:** your main requirement is a specialized Apple calendar interface or you would need to move your calendar provider solely to use Outlook.

Check a shared work calendar, an invitation response and an event created from your phone. Also confirm which account owns newly created appointments. A successful login alone is not sufficient migration evidence.

## 4. BusyCal: more control over the Mac calendar

BusyCal is an alternative for Mac users who care about views, density, event detail and filtering. Its product page describes customizable calendar views, tasks and smart filters. The value is control over how a complicated calendar appears, rather than moving all events into a different service. [BusyCal for Mac](https://www.busymac.com/busycal/)

A consultant handling several work calendars can evaluate whether a tailored view makes the next appointment easier to identify. Choose it because the configurable interface solves a concrete problem, not because a perpetual license sounds universally cheaper.

**Cost:** the direct Mac purchase is $49.99 USD. BusyMac explains that its direct perpetual license includes 18 months of updates; continued use of the last eligible version does not require renewal, while later updates can require an optional paid renewal. App Store licensing uses a different model. [BusyMac licensing](https://www.busymac.com/docs/faqs/120616-licensing-explained/)

The iPhone, iPad and Apple Watch app is a separate $9.99 USD one-time purchase. Do not treat the Mac price as an all-device bundle. [BusyCal for iOS](https://www.busymac.com/busycalios/)

**Tradeoff:** the Apple focus and separate purchases may outweigh licensing benefits for a mixed-device user. Keeping an older version indefinitely also differs from receiving every future update.

**Skip it if:** Windows or Android is a required native platform, or you mainly need the booking workflow already configured in Fantastical.

## 5. Calendars by Readdle: events and tasks on Apple devices

Calendars by Readdle is relevant when you want another Apple calendar interface with tasks beside appointments. Its current product page describes Google, Outlook, Exchange and iCloud connections, together with natural-language creation. [Calendars by Readdle](https://readdle.com/calendars)

The useful evaluation is personal: can you recognize free time, add a task and distinguish work from home appointments without extra steps? A product's planning vocabulary does not establish that it will suit your habits.

**Cost:** free core functionality and paid options are advertised. Confirm the actual offer inside your current app version and region. Readdle's older help page places several features in PRO and quotes an annual rate, while the current homepage describes a broader core offer. That conflict prevents a reliable current price-and-entitlement comparison. [Readdle plan help](https://apphelp.readdle.com/en/index.php?id=1584&pg=kb.page)

An official lifetime announcement also says some major future updates may cost extra. A lifetime label therefore needs its purchase terms checked, rather than being interpreted as every future feature forever. [Lifetime purchase explanation](https://readdle.com/blog/calendars-lifetime-purchase-option)

**Tradeoff:** Apple availability does not address a requirement for one native Windows-and-Android client. Do not assume every connection or paid feature behaves identically on Mac and iPhone.

**Skip it if:** a specific cross-platform requirement is why you are leaving Fantastical, or you cannot establish which plan contains your essential task and calendar features.

## 6. Morgen: move toward scheduling work, not just viewing events

Morgen is relevant if the missing piece is turning tasks into a workable schedule. It combines calendars and task integrations with time blocking and an AI planner. This is a broader planning purchase than replacing Fantastical's calendar interface. [Morgen overview](https://www.morgen.so/)

For example, a person who knows every meeting time but continually leaves tasks outside the calendar may benefit from a tool designed around placing work into available slots. If the problem is only subscription cost, this is unlikely to be the first option to evaluate.

**Cost:** individual plans are $30 per month billed monthly, or $15 per month billed yearly, totaling $180 for the annual commitment. A 14-day trial is advertised. Team offers have separate seat requirements and are not the price for one individual. [Morgen pricing](https://www.morgen.so/pricing)

**Tradeoff:** task connectors, scheduling rules and another planning workflow introduce configuration and learning work. Desktop, mobile and browser availability does not prove identical functionality on each device.

**Skip it if:** you only need faster appointment entry and a familiar calendar, or you want to avoid another substantial recurring commitment.

Test the task sources you actually use. Putting a task-shaped event on a calendar is different from updating the original task's completion state.

## Move without duplicating your calendars

First list where your data lives: calendar providers, local calendars, task services and Fantastical-specific settings. Connect the same provider accounts to the replacement before considering an import. Check whether events are already present.

For locally stored calendar events, Apple documents individual-calendar export as ICS and a complete calendar archive as ICBU. These are different formats; an archive is not automatically accepted by every replacement. Exporting calendar events also does not transfer task-service data, calendar sets or booking templates. [Apple calendar export](https://support.apple.com/guide/calendar/import-or-export-calendars-icl1023/mac)

Evaluate one recurring event across a time-zone change, one shared calendar and one invitation response. Keep one source of truth. Avoid copying entire calendars into another account merely to make a second client display them.

Recreate availability rules and replace public booking links only after checking the new destination. Review notification settings on both clients so reminders do not fire twice. Cancel a subscription after confirming the replacement covers your real workweek.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is a free Fantastical alternative for Apple users?

Apple Calendar is the included option to evaluate first. Google Calendar and personal Outlook also provide free starting points, but their account ecosystems and interfaces differ. Fantastical itself has a free edition, so check whether switching is necessary.

### Is Fantastical available on Windows?

Yes. Flexibits currently lists Windows support. Compare the particular Windows features you need before replacing it because of an outdated platform claim.

### Which option avoids a mandatory calendar-app subscription?

BusyCal's direct Mac license is perpetual, with an included update period and optional renewal afterward. Its App Store model differs, and the mobile app is separate. Apple Calendar is also an included baseline.

### Can I keep my Google or iCloud account?

Often the change is a client change: you reconnect a supported account and keep its events there. Confirm that the chosen client supports your provider, permissions and required devices. Do not assume every calendar visible in an app is editable.

### Will exporting events also move Fantastical settings?

No complete settings transfer was verified. Calendar exports should be treated as event data, with task integrations, interface preferences and booking templates handled separately.

### Which alternative is best for scheduling tasks?

Morgen is the planning-focused candidate in this shortlist. Evaluate its actual task integration and cost against your needs. If you only want to view appointments, start with a simpler calendar.

## Choose one replacement to evaluate

Start with the account-and-device combination you cannot compromise on. Compare its free baseline with one specialized candidate, using your real shared calendars and invitations. Keep Fantastical if its current workflow remains easier and the premium features justify the bill. Switch when the alternative solves a defined problem and the account checks pass.
